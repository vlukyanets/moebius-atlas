## Why It Matters

$(x - 3)(x + 3)$: multiply it out and the middle terms vanish - you're
left with $x^2 - 9$. It happens every time a difference meets the
matching sum, and it's the most useful of all the short
multiplication formulas: it computes $49 \cdot 51$ instantly, it
factors $a^2 - b^2$ in one move, and later it clears square roots from
denominators. Two brackets in, two terms out.

## The Big Idea

$(a - b)(a + b)$ by the polynomial rule: $a \cdot a + a \cdot b - b \cdot a -
b \cdot b$. The two middle products are $ab$ and $-ab$ - opposites - so
they cancel, leaving $a^2 - b^2$. The picture: a square $a \times a$ with
a corner $b \times b$ cut out can be re-cut into a rectangle $(a - b)$
by $(a + b)$.

Numbers use it best. $49 \cdot 51 = (50 - 1)(50 + 1) = 2500 - 1 = 2499$.
Whenever two numbers sit the same distance either side of a round
number, their product is the round number squared minus the distance
squared.

## The Rule

**The product of the difference of two expressions and their sum
equals the difference of their squares:**

$$
(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

It's an identity, for any expressions $a$ and $b$. The order of the
brackets doesn't matter: $(a + b)(a - b)$ gives the same. But the *same*
two expressions must appear in both brackets, one with a minus and
one with a plus.

## Worked Example

Expand $(x - 3)(x + 3)$, $(2m + 5n)(2m - 5n)$ and $(a^2 - 1)(a^2 + 1)$;
compute $49 \cdot 51$ and $10.2 \cdot 9.8$.

- **$(x - 3)(x + 3) = x^2 - 9$.**
- **$(2m + 5n)(2m - 5n) = (2m)^2 - (5n)^2 = 4m^2 - 25n^2$.**
- **$(a^2 - 1)(a^2 + 1) = a^4 - 1$.**
- **$49 \cdot 51 = (50 - 1)(50 + 1) = 2500 - 1 = 2499$.**
- **$10.2 \cdot 9.8 = (10 + 0.2)(10 - 0.2) = 100 - 0.04 = 99.96$.**

A trickier one: $(-x - 4)(4 - x)$. Rewrite as $-(x + 4)(4 - x) = -(4 +
x)(4 - x) = -(16 - x^2) = x^2 - 16$.

## Common Mistake

Applying the formula to $(a + b)(a + b)$ or $(a - b)(a - b)$. Those are
*squares*, and they have three terms: $(x + 3)(x + 3) = x^2 + 6x + 9$, not
$x^2 - 9$. The middle terms only cancel when one bracket has a minus
and the other a plus. Check the signs before writing two terms.

## The Takeaway

Difference times sum is the difference of the squares: $(a - b)(a + b) =
a^2 - b^2$, two terms, no middle. Use it for mental arithmetic with
numbers either side of a round one, and for the factoring that comes
next.

## Check Yourself

1. Expand $(y - 8)(y + 8)$.
2. Expand $(3a - b)(3a + b)$.
3. Expand $(0.5 + x)(0.5 - x)$.
4. Compute $79 \cdot 81$ and $1003 \cdot 997$.
5. Simplify $(x - 1)(x + 1) - x^2$.
6. Someone expands $(t + 6)(t + 6)$ as $t^2 - 36$. What's the right
   answer, and why?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $y^2 - 64$.
> 2. $9a^2 - b^2$.
> 3. $0.25 - x^2$.
> 4. $6400 - 1 = 6399$; $1\,000\,000 - 9 = 999\,991$.
> 5. $-1$.
> 6. $t^2 + 12t + 36$ - both brackets have a plus, so it's a square of a
>    sum, not a difference of squares.
