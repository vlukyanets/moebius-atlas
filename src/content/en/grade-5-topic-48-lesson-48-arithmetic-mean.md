## Why It Matters

Your marks this term were $9$, $11$, $10$, $12$ and $8$ - what's your
average? A shop sold $120$, $95$, $130$ and $115$ ice creams on four days
- what's a typical day? A car covered $270$ km in $4.5$ hours - what was
its average speed? Whenever a pile of different numbers has to be summed
up in one, that one number is almost always the arithmetic mean. It's
the most-used number in sports, school and the news.

## The Big Idea

Five glasses hold different amounts of water. Pour all of it into a jug,
then share it back out equally - every glass now holds the same amount,
and that amount is the mean. Nothing was added or lost; the total is the
same as before, just levelled out. That's exactly the calculation: add
everything up, then divide by how many there were.

The mean always lands somewhere between the smallest and the largest
value - it can't be above every glass, or below every glass, because
it's what they'd all be if they were equal.

## The Rule

The **arithmetic mean** of several numbers is their sum divided by how
many of them there are:

$$
\text{mean} = \frac{\text{sum of the numbers}}{\text{how many numbers}}
$$

For $9, 11, 10, 12, 8$:

$$
\frac{9 + 11 + 10 + 12 + 8}{5} = \frac{50}{5} = 10
$$

The mean need not be one of the numbers, and it need not be a natural
number - it's often a decimal.

The **average value** of a quantity is found the same way. Average speed
is the whole distance divided by the whole time; average price is the
whole cost divided by the whole quantity. Turned around: the sum equals
the mean times the count.

## Worked Example

A shop sold $120$, $95$, $130$ and $115$ ice creams on four days. Find
the mean daily sales.

- **Add:** $120 + 95 + 130 + 115 = 460$.
- **Count:** four days.
- **Divide:** $460 : 4 = 115$.

So the mean is $115$ a day. Check that it's between the smallest ($95$)
and the largest ($130$) - it is.

Now average speed. A car covers $270$ km in $4.5$ h: $270 : 4.5 = 2700 :
45 = 60$ km/h. That's what the speed would have been if the car had
driven steadily the whole way.

And backwards: the mean of three numbers is $7.4$, two of them are $6.1$
and $8.9$. The sum is $7.4 \cdot 3 = 22.2$, so the third is $22.2 - 6.1 -
8.9 = 7.2$.

## Common Mistake

Averaging speeds instead of distances and times. A car goes $60$ km/h
for the first hour and $40$ km/h for the next two hours: the average
speed is not $\frac{60 + 40}{2} = 50$. It covered $60 + 80 = 140$ km in
$3$ h, so the average speed is $140 : 3 \approx 46.7$ km/h. Always divide
the *whole* distance by the *whole* time. The same goes for average
price: total cost over total quantity, not the mean of the price tags.

## The Takeaway

The arithmetic mean is the sum divided by the count - the value
everything would have if it were levelled out. It always sits between the
smallest and largest values, and an average speed or price is always
whole amount over whole amount.

## Check Yourself

1. Find the mean of $14$, $9$, $16$ and $13$.
2. Find the mean of $2.5$, $3.7$ and $4$.
3. A pupil's marks are $10$, $8$, $11$, $11$, $9$, $11$. What's the
   average mark?
4. A cyclist rode $54$ km in $3$ h and then $36$ km in $2$ h. Find the
   average speed for the whole trip.
5. The mean of four numbers is $12$. What's their sum?
6. Someone says the mean of $5$, $5$, $5$ and $25$ is "$5$, because most
   of them are $5$." Find the actual mean.

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $52 : 4 = 13$.
> 2. $10.2 : 3 = 3.4$.
> 3. $60 : 6 = 10$.
> 4. $90$ km in $5$ h: $18$ km/h.
> 5. $12 \cdot 4 = 48$.
> 6. $40 : 4 = 10$ - the mean is the levelled-out value, not the commonest
>    one.
