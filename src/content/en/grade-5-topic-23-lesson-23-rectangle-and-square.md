## Why It Matters

Look around: the page, the screen, the door, the window, the table top,
the tiles on the floor. Almost everything people make is a rectangle - it
stacks, it fits against walls, and it's easy to cut. Knowing how long the
edge of one is, all the way round, is the first question anyone building
a frame, a fence or a picture border has to answer.

## The Big Idea

A rectangle is a four-sided shape where every corner is a right angle -
the corner of a page, four times. That one rule forces something useful:
the sides across from each other come out equal. So a rectangle doesn't
need four numbers, only two - how long and how wide - and everything else
follows. Walk round it: long side, short side, long side, short side.
That's two of each, which is why the perimeter is twice the sum.

A square is a rectangle that's given up its last freedom: all four sides
the same. One number describes it completely.

## The Rule

A **rectangle** is a quadrilateral - a figure with four sides - in which
all four angles are right angles. Its **opposite sides are equal**: in
the rectangle $ABCD$, $AB = CD$ and $BC = AD$. The two different side
lengths are its **length** $a$ and **width** $b$.

The perimeter of a rectangle:

$$
P = 2 \cdot (a + b) = 2a + 2b
$$

A **square** is a rectangle with all four sides equal. With side $a$:

$$
P = 4a
$$

A segment joining two opposite vertices, like $AC$, is a **diagonal**. A
rectangle has two, and they're equal.

## Worked Example

A rectangle is $12$ cm long and $7$ cm wide. Find its perimeter.

- **Name the sides:** $a = 12$, $b = 7$.
- **Add them:** $a + b = 19$ cm - that's one long side and one short side,
  half of the way round.
- **Double it:** $P = 2 \cdot 19 = 38$ cm.

Now backwards. A rectangle has perimeter $50$ cm and width $9$ cm. Find
the length. Half the perimeter is $50 : 2 = 25$ cm, and that's $a + b$, so
$a = 25 - 9 = 16$ cm.

And a square with perimeter $36$ cm has side $36 : 4 = 9$ cm.

## Common Mistake

Adding the length and the width and stopping. $12 + 7 = 19$ is only two
of the four sides - halfway round the rectangle. The other two sides are
the same again, so the perimeter is $38$. If the formula $2(a + b)$ ever
slips your mind, walk round the shape and name each side as you go:
$12 + 7 + 12 + 7$.

## The Takeaway

A rectangle has four right angles and equal opposite sides, so two numbers
describe it and its perimeter is twice their sum. A square is a rectangle
with all sides equal, and its perimeter is four times the side.

## Check Yourself

1. Find the perimeter of a rectangle with sides $15$ cm and $8$ cm.
2. Find the perimeter of a square with side $23$ cm.
3. A rectangle has perimeter $64$ cm and length $20$ cm. Find the width.
4. A square has perimeter $100$ cm. Find its side.
5. In the rectangle $KLMN$, $KL = 6$ cm and $LM = 4$ cm. Find $MN$ and
   $NK$.
6. Someone says a rectangle $12$ cm by $7$ cm has perimeter $19$ cm.
   What did they forget?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $2 \cdot (15 + 8) = 46$ cm.
> 2. $4 \cdot 23 = 92$ cm.
> 3. $64 : 2 - 20 = 12$ cm.
> 4. $100 : 4 = 25$ cm.
> 5. $MN = KL = 6$ cm, $NK = LM = 4$ cm - opposite sides are equal.
> 6. The other two sides: $19$ is half the perimeter; the whole is
>    $38$ cm.
