## Why It Matters

How does a family spend its money? What share of the class walks to
school, and what share takes the bus? What's the air made of? Whenever
one whole is split into parts and you want to *see* the sizes of the
shares, a bar chart shows the numbers but a pie chart shows the whole -
and how big a bite each part takes out of it.

## The Big Idea

A pie chart is a disc cut into sectors, one per part, with the whole
disc standing for $100\%$ - or for the total, whatever it is. A part
that's a quarter of the total gets a quarter of the disc: a sector of
$90^\circ$. In general, the sector's angle is the same fraction of
$360^\circ$ as the part is of the whole. That's the sector rule from the
last topic, run backwards: instead of finding a sector's area from its
angle, you find the angle from the share.

## The Rule

A **pie chart** shows how a whole is divided into parts: the whole is a
disc, each part is a sector, and the sector's central angle is
proportional to the part's share of the whole.

To draw one:

1. find each part's share of the whole - as a fraction or a percent;
2. multiply by $360^\circ$ to get each sector's angle;
3. draw the sectors with a protractor; the angles should add up to
   $360^\circ$.

$$
\text{angle} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \cdot 360^\circ = \text{percent} \cdot 3.6^\circ
$$

To read one: measure or estimate a sector's angle, divide by
$360^\circ$, and that's the part's share.

## Worked Example

Of $40$ pupils, $20$ walk to school, $12$ take the bus and $8$ are driven.
Draw a pie chart.

- **Shares:** walk $\frac{20}{40} = \frac{1}{2}$, bus $\frac{12}{40} =
  \frac{3}{10}$, car $\frac{8}{40} = \frac{1}{5}$.
- **Angles:** $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 360^\circ = 180^\circ$; $\frac{3}{10}
  \cdot 360^\circ = 108^\circ$; $\frac{1}{5} \cdot 360^\circ = 72^\circ$.
- **Check:** $180 + 108 + 72 = 360$. Draw a half-disc for walking, then
  sectors of $108^\circ$ and $72^\circ$.

Reading one: a chart of a family budget has a "food" sector of
$126^\circ$. Its share is $126 : 360 = 0.35 = 35\%$. If the budget is
$20\,000$ hryvnias, food is $20\,000 \cdot 0.35 = 7000$.

## Common Mistake

Drawing the sector angles as the percents themselves. A $35\%$ share is
not a $35^\circ$ sector - that's less than a tenth of the disc. Percents
are hundredths, and the disc has $360$ degrees, so each percent is
$3.6^\circ$: $35\%$ is $126^\circ$. Convert, and check the angles total
$360^\circ$.

## The Takeaway

A pie chart is a disc cut into sectors whose angles are proportional to
the parts of a whole: share times $360^\circ$, or percent times
$3.6^\circ$. It shows at a glance how big each part is compared with the
whole - the one thing a bar chart doesn't.

## Check Yourself

1. What angle does a $25\%$ share get? A $10\%$ share?
2. A share is $\frac{2}{9}$ of the whole. Find its angle.
3. A sector is $45^\circ$. What fraction of the whole is it? What
   percent?
4. A class of $30$ has $18$ girls and $12$ boys. Find the sector angles.
5. A budget chart has sectors of $90^\circ$, $144^\circ$ and $126^\circ$.
   What percent is each?
6. Someone draws a $40\%$ share as a $40^\circ$ sector. What should it
   be?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $90^\circ$; $36^\circ$.
> 2. $80^\circ$.
> 3. $\frac{1}{8}$, or $12.5\%$.
> 4. Girls $216^\circ$, boys $144^\circ$.
> 5. $25\%$, $40\%$, $35\%$.
> 6. $40 \cdot 3.6^\circ = 144^\circ$.
