## Why It Matters

$x^2 + 5x - 3$. $2ab - b^2 + a^2$. $t^3 - t$. Nearly every formula you'll
meet in physics, finance or geometry is a sum of monomials - a
polynomial. The areas of composite shapes, the height of a thrown ball,
the cost of a phone plan: all polynomials. Learning to write them
tidily, name their parts and know their degree is the vocabulary for
everything that follows.

## The Big Idea

Glue monomials together with plus and minus signs and you have a
polynomial; each monomial is a term. Some terms may be alike - same
letter part - and those can be collected, exactly as in grade 6. Once
every term is in standard form and no like terms remain, the
polynomial is in standard form too: as short as it can be written.

Its degree is the highest degree among its terms. $x^2 + 5x - 3$ has
degree $2$ because of the $x^2$; $2ab - b^2 + a^2$ has degree $2$ because
each term does; $t^3 - t$ has degree $3$.

## The Rule

A **polynomial** is a sum of monomials. The monomials are its
**terms**. A polynomial with two terms is a **binomial**, with three a
**trinomial**; a monomial counts as a polynomial with one term.

**Like terms** of a polynomial are terms with the same letter part.
**Collecting** them means adding their coefficients.

A polynomial is in **standard form** if every term is a monomial in
standard form and there are no like terms. Terms are usually ordered
by descending degree.

The **degree** of a polynomial in standard form is the largest degree
of its terms.

## Worked Example

Bring $3x^2 - 5x + 2x \cdot x + 7 - 8x$ to standard form and give its
degree. Then do the same for $a^2b - 3ab + 2ab^2 + 4ab - a^2b$.

- **Tidy each term:** $2x \cdot x = 2x^2$. So: $3x^2 - 5x + 2x^2 + 7 - 8x$.
- **Like terms:** $3x^2 + 2x^2 = 5x^2$; $-5x - 8x = -13x$; $7$ alone.
- **Standard form:** $5x^2 - 13x + 7$. Degree $2$ - a trinomial.
- **Second:** $a^2b - a^2b = 0$ - they cancel; $-3ab + 4ab = ab$; $2ab^2$
  alone. Standard form: $2ab^2 + ab$. Degree $3$ (from $2ab^2$).

Name the parts of $-4y^3 + y - 6$: terms $-4y^3$, $y$, $-6$; coefficients
$-4$, $1$, $-6$; degree $3$; a trinomial.

## Common Mistake

Reading the degree off the first term, or off the number of terms.
$x + x^5 - 2$ has degree $5$, not $1$ - the terms weren't in descending
order. And a trinomial isn't "degree $3$": $x^2 + x + 1$ is a trinomial
of degree $2$. Find the largest exponent total among the terms, after
collecting - because like terms can cancel and take the top degree
with them.

## The Takeaway

A polynomial is a sum of monomials; tidy each term, collect like terms,
and you have the standard form. The degree is the highest degree of a
term in that form.

## Check Yourself

1. Bring $2a - 7 + 5a - 3 + a$ to standard form.
2. Bring $x^3 - 2x^2 + 4x^2 - x^3 + x$ to standard form and give the
   degree.
3. Is $3m^2n - 5mn^2$ a binomial or a trinomial? What's its degree?
4. Which terms are like: $4xy$, $-xy^2$, $2yx$, $xy$?
5. Write the polynomial for the perimeter of a rectangle with sides
   $2x + 1$ and $x - 3$, in standard form.
6. Someone says $y + y^4$ has degree $1$ "because it starts with $y$."
   What's the degree?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $8a - 10$.
> 2. $2x^2 + x$; degree $2$.
> 3. A binomial of degree $3$.
> 4. $4xy$, $2yx$, $xy$.
> 5. $2(2x + 1) + 2(x - 3) = 6x - 4$.
> 6. $4$ - the highest degree among the terms.
