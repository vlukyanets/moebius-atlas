## Why It Matters

Windows, screens, fields, kites, the faces of a box - four-sided
shapes are the most common ones people make, and this year's geometry
is largely about them. The triangle was the atom; the quadrilateral is
the first molecule, and the first thing to know is that it's two
triangles glued along a diagonal - which is why its angles add up to
twice a triangle's.

## The Big Idea

Four points, no three on a line, joined in a cycle: four sides, four
vertices, four angles. The two segments joining opposite corners are
the diagonals, and drawing one cuts the quadrilateral into two
triangles. Each triangle contributes $180^\circ$, and their six angles
make up exactly the four angles of the quadrilateral - so those add to
$360^\circ$. That one fact does for quadrilaterals what the angle sum
did for triangles.

A quadrilateral is convex if it lies on one side of every line
through a side - no dent. Convex ones are the ones this year studies,
and all the special kinds (parallelogram, trapezoid, rectangle, rhombus,
square) are convex.

## The Rule

A **quadrilateral** $ABCD$ is a figure made of four points $A$, $B$, $C$,
$D$ - its **vertices**, no three on a line - and four segments $AB$, $BC$,
$CD$, $DA$ - its **sides** - joining them in order, with no two sides
crossing. Vertices are named in order round the figure.

**Adjacent** sides share a vertex ($AB$, $BC$); **opposite** sides don't
($AB$, $CD$). Likewise adjacent and opposite vertices and angles. The
**diagonals** are $AC$ and $BD$. The **perimeter** is the sum of the
sides.

A quadrilateral is **convex** if it lies on one side of each line
containing a side. A convex quadrilateral's diagonals intersect.

**Theorem.** The sum of the angles of a convex quadrilateral is
$360^\circ$.

*Proof.* The diagonal $AC$ splits $ABCD$ into $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle
ACD$; their angle sums, $180^\circ$ each, together make up the four
angles of the quadrilateral. $\square$

## Worked Example

In quadrilateral $ABCD$, $\angle A = 70^\circ$, $\angle B = 110^\circ$, $\angle C =
85^\circ$. Find $\angle D$. Then: the angles of a quadrilateral are in the
ratio $1 : 2 : 3 : 4$ - find them. Then: name the sides opposite $AB$ and
adjacent to it.

- **$\angle D = 360^\circ - 70^\circ - 110^\circ - 85^\circ = 95^\circ$.**
- **Ratio:** $x + 2x + 3x + 4x = 360$, $x = 36$: $36^\circ$, $72^\circ$,
  $108^\circ$, $144^\circ$.
- **Opposite $AB$:** $CD$. **Adjacent to $AB$:** $BC$ and $DA$.

Perimeter: sides $3$, $5$, $4$, $6$ give $18$; and the diagonals of a
convex quadrilateral - there are two, and they cross.

## Common Mistake

Naming the vertices out of order. $ABCD$ means $A$ to $B$ to $C$ to $D$
and back; "$ACBD$" for the same four points describes a different
figure - a crossed one, with $AC$ and $BD$ as sides. The name fixes
which pairs are sides and which are diagonals. And the $360^\circ$ is for
*convex* quadrilaterals; a dented one still has the sum $360^\circ$, but
one "angle" exceeds $180^\circ$ and the diagonal argument needs care.

## The Takeaway

A quadrilateral is four vertices in order, four sides, two diagonals;
one diagonal cuts it into two triangles, so its angles total
$360^\circ$. Sides and angles come in adjacent and opposite pairs, and
the vertex order in the name tells which is which.

## Check Yourself

1. Three angles of a quadrilateral are $90^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $120^\circ$.
   Find the fourth.
2. Can a quadrilateral have angles $100^\circ$, $100^\circ$, $100^\circ$,
   $100^\circ$?
3. In $PQRS$, name the diagonals and the side opposite $QR$.
4. The angles of a quadrilateral are $x$, $x + 20^\circ$, $x + 40^\circ$, $x +
   60^\circ$. Find $x$.
5. A quadrilateral has perimeter $40$ and three sides of $9$, $11$, $8$.
   Find the fourth.
6. Someone says the four angles of a quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$
   "like a triangle." What's the sum, and why?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $60^\circ$.
> 2. No - the sum would be $400^\circ$.
> 3. $PR$ and $QS$; $SP$.
> 4. $4x + 120 = 360$, $x = 60^\circ$.
> 5. $12$.
> 6. $360^\circ$ - a diagonal splits it into two triangles, $180^\circ$ each.
