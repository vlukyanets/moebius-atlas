## Why It Matters

The thermometer says $-7^\circ$. A bank account is $-250$ hryvnias
overdrawn. A submarine is at $-300$ m. The lift stops at floor $-2$. Up
to now, numbers only counted and measured what was *there*; but the
world also has "below zero," "owed" and "underground," and it needs
numbers for those too. Negative numbers are how the number line grows a
left-hand half.

## The Big Idea

A thermometer is a coordinate ray with one thing added: it keeps going
below $0$. Every degree above zero has a twin below it, marked with a
minus sign - $7$ and $-7$ are the same distance from zero, on opposite
sides. Zero itself is neither above nor below; it's the boundary.

The minus sign in $-7$ isn't a subtraction - there's nothing to subtract
from. It's part of the number's name, saying which side of zero it's
on. Numbers on the right, the old ones, get a $+$ that's usually left
off; numbers on the left get a $-$ that never is.

## The Rule

Numbers greater than zero are **positive**: $7$, $2.5$, $\frac{1}{3}$ -
written with a plus sign or, usually, with no sign at all. Numbers less
than zero are **negative**: $-7$, $-2.5$, $-\frac{1}{3}$ - always written
with a minus sign.

**Zero is neither positive nor negative.** It separates the two kinds.

A number's sign tells which side of zero it's on; the digits tell how
far. So $-7$ is seven units to the left of zero, on a line where $7$ is
seven units to the right.

Everyday readings: temperature above/below freezing, height above/below
sea level, money owned/owed, time after/before an event - each pairs a
positive with a negative.

## Worked Example

The temperature at midnight was $-4^\circ$. By noon it had risen by
$9$ degrees. What was the temperature at noon?

- **Picture the thermometer.** $-4$ is four marks below zero.
- **Go up nine marks.** Four of them bring you to $0$; the other five
  take you to $5$.
- **Noon:** $5^\circ$ - five degrees above zero.

Now a sign question. Write as a number with a sign: $300$ m below sea
level; a debt of $250$ hryvnias; $12^\circ$ of frost; $3$ floors up. Below
sea level $-300$; debt $-250$; frost $-12$; up $+3$, or just $3$.

And a trick question: which is colder, $-2^\circ$ or $-9^\circ$? Nine
below is further below, so $-9^\circ$ is colder.

## Common Mistake

Treating the minus sign as an instruction to subtract something. "$-7$"
is a complete number, not "seven taken away" - taken away from what? It
lives on the number line seven steps left of zero, as real as $7$. And
zero isn't "a small positive number": it has no sign at all. A
temperature of $0^\circ$ is neither warm nor frosty.

## The Takeaway

Positive numbers sit to the right of zero, negative ones to the left,
and zero is the boundary that belongs to neither. The minus sign is
part of the number's name - it says which side, and the rest says how
far.

## Check Yourself

1. Write with a sign: $5$ degrees of frost; $150$ m above sea level; a
   loss of $40$ hryvnias.
2. Which of these are negative: $-3$, $0$, $2.5$, $-0.1$, $\frac{1}{2}$?
3. Is zero positive, negative, or neither?
4. The temperature was $-6^\circ$ and rose by $4$ degrees. What is it
   now?
5. The temperature was $3^\circ$ and fell by $8$ degrees. What is it now?
6. Someone says $-15^\circ$ is warmer than $-3^\circ$ "because $15$ is
   bigger than $3$." What's wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $-5$; $+150$ (or $150$); $-40$.
> 2. $-3$ and $-0.1$.
> 3. Neither.
> 4. $-2^\circ$.
> 5. $-5^\circ$.
> 6. Fifteen *below* zero is further below than three below; $-15^\circ$
>    is colder.
