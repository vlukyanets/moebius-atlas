## Why It Matters

Twice as many workers, half the time. Twice the speed, half the journey
time. Twice as many people sharing a cake, half a slice each. Some
pairs of quantities move in opposite directions in lockstep: scale one
up by a factor and the other scales *down* by the same factor. It's the
mirror image of direct proportionality, and it's just as common - and
just as easy to get backwards.

## The Big Idea

A $120$-km trip at $60$ km/h takes $2$ hours; at $120$ km/h, $1$ hour; at
$30$ km/h, $4$ hours. Double the speed and the time halves; halve the
speed and the time doubles. What stays fixed isn't the ratio of speed
to time - it's their *product*: $60 \cdot 2 = 120 \cdot 1 = 30 \cdot 4 = 120$,
the length of the trip. Two quantities are inversely proportional when
their product is constant.

Direct: the ratio is constant, and the graph is a straight line through
the origin. Inverse: the product is constant, and one goes up as the
other comes down.

## The Rule

Two quantities are **inversely proportional** if, when one of them is
multiplied by some number, the other is *divided* by the same number.
Equivalently, their product is a constant:

$$
x \cdot y = k, \qquad \text{or} \qquad y = \frac{k}{x}
$$

For inversely proportional quantities, two pairs of values satisfy
$x_1 y_1 = x_2 y_2$, which can be written as a proportion with one ratio
*flipped*:

$$
\frac{y_1}{y_2} = \frac{x_2}{x_1}
$$

## Worked Example

$6$ workers can tile a floor in $10$ hours. How long would $4$ workers
take, working at the same rate?

- **Check the kind of proportionality.** Fewer workers, more time - by
  the same factor. Inverse.
- **Use the constant product:** $6 \cdot 10 = 60$ worker-hours of work in
  total. $4$ workers need $60 : 4 = 15$ hours.
- **Or the flipped proportion:** $\frac{10}{x} = \frac{4}{6}$, so
  $4x = 60$, $x = 15$.

They'd take $15$ hours.

Now the trip: $120$ km at $80$ km/h. Product $120$; time $120 : 80 = 1.5$
hours.

And a non-example: a car's fuel use per $100$ km and the distance it
can go on a full tank *are* inversely proportional; but the number of
pages read and the pages left in a book are not - their *sum* is
constant, not their product.

## Common Mistake

Setting up an inverse problem as a direct proportion. "$6$ workers take
$10$ hours, so $4$ workers take $x$": writing $\frac{6}{4} = \frac{10}{x}$
gives $x \approx 6.7$ hours - fewer workers finishing *sooner*, which is
absurd. In an inverse relationship one ratio must be flipped, or,
simpler, use the constant product. Ask first: does more of this mean
more or less of that?

## The Takeaway

Inversely proportional quantities have a constant product: double one,
halve the other. Solve with $x_1 y_1 = x_2 y_2$, or a proportion with one
ratio flipped - and always decide first whether the relationship is
direct or inverse.

## Check Yourself

1. $8$ pumps empty a pool in $6$ hours. How long do $12$ pumps take?
2. A trip takes $3$ hours at $90$ km/h. How long at $60$ km/h?
3. $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$ with $k = 24$. Find $y$ when
   $x = 3$, and $x$ when $y = 4$.
4. Direct, inverse or neither: the price per kilogram and the kilograms
   you can buy for $200$ hryvnias?
5. Direct, inverse or neither: the number of pupils in a class and the
   number of desks needed?
6. Someone says $4$ workers finish in $6.7$ hours what $6$ workers finish
   in $10$. Why is that wrong, and what's the answer?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $8 \cdot 6 = 48$; $48 : 12 = 4$ hours.
> 2. $270 : 60 = 4.5$ hours.
> 3. $y = 8$; $x = 6$.
> 4. Inverse - the product is $200$.
> 5. Direct.
> 6. Fewer workers can't be faster; the product is $60$, so $15$ hours.
