## Why It Matters

Every equation you've solved so far ends the same way: something times
$x$ equals a number. $3x = 15$, $-2x = 7$, $0 \cdot x = 4$. That final
shape has a name - a linear equation - and it's worth studying on its
own, because it's the destination every equation of this course is
driven to, and its answer depends entirely on two numbers.

## The Big Idea

$ax = b$ says "$a$ copies of $x$ make $b$." If $a$ isn't zero, there's
exactly one $x$ that works: $b : a$. That's the ordinary case. But if
$a$ *is* zero, the left side is $0$ no matter what $x$ is - and then
either $b$ is also $0$, and every $x$ works, or $b$ isn't, and nothing
does. Three cases, decided by looking at $a$ and $b$ before you divide.

Any equation that can be pushed into the form $ax = b$ by the two safe
moves is really a linear equation in disguise, whatever it looked like
at the start.

## The Rule

A **linear equation in one variable** is an equation of the form

$$
ax = b
$$

where $x$ is the variable and $a$, $b$ are numbers.

- If $a \neq 0$: **one root**, $x = \frac{b}{a}$.
- If $a = 0$ and $b = 0$: the equation is $0 \cdot x = 0$ - **every
  number** is a root.
- If $a = 0$ and $b \neq 0$: the equation is $0 \cdot x = b$ - **no
  roots**.

Equations that reduce to $ax = b$ by transposing terms, expanding
brackets, collecting like terms and multiplying by a non-zero number
are also called linear, and are solved by reducing them to that form.

## Worked Example

Solve $-2x = 7$, $0 \cdot x = 0$, $0 \cdot x = -3$, and $5(x - 2) - 3x =
2(x + 1) - 12$.

- **$-2x = 7$.** $a = -2 \neq 0$: one root, $x = 7 : (-2) = -3.5$.
- **$0 \cdot x = 0$.** $a = 0$, $b = 0$: every number is a root.
- **$0 \cdot x = -3$.** $a = 0$, $b = -3$: no roots.
- **$5(x - 2) - 3x = 2(x + 1) - 12$.** Expand: $5x - 10 - 3x = 2x + 2 - 12$.
  Collect: $2x - 10 = 2x - 10$. Transpose: $2x - 2x = -10 + 10$, that is
  $0 \cdot x = 0$. Every number is a root.

With a parameter: for which $a$ does $ax = 8$ have no roots? Only when
$a = 0$ - then it reads $0 = 8$.

## Common Mistake

Dividing by $a$ without checking that it isn't zero. Reducing
$5(x - 2) - 3x = 2(x + 1) - 12$ to $0 \cdot x = 0$ and then "dividing by
$0$" to get $x = 0$ - one root - is wrong on two counts: you can't
divide by zero, and the true answer is *every* number. When the $x$'s
cancel, stop and look at what's left: $0 = 0$ means all, $0 = $
something else means none.

## The Takeaway

A linear equation is $ax = b$. With $a \neq 0$ it has exactly one root,
$\frac{b}{a}$; with $a = 0$ it has all numbers or none, depending on $b$.
Reduce, look at $a$, then decide.

## Check Yourself

1. Solve $4x = -18$.
2. Solve $-x = 0.6$.
3. Solve $3(x + 4) = 3x + 12$.
4. Solve $2(x - 1) = 2x + 5$.
5. Solve $7x - 3(2x + 1) = x - 3$.
6. For which values of $a$ does $ax = 0$ have exactly one root? For
   which does it have infinitely many?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $x = -4.5$.
> 2. $x = -0.6$.
> 3. Every number.
> 4. No roots ($-2 = 5$).
> 5. $x - 3 = x - 3$: every number.
> 6. $a \neq 0$ (root $0$); $a = 0$.
