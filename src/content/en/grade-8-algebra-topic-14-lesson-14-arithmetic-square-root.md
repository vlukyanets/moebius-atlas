## Why It Matters

A square has area $2$ square metres - what's its side? A number that
squares to $2$. There is one, but it isn't a fraction and it isn't a
terminating decimal: it begins $1.41421\ldots$ and never repeats.
Numbers like this need a symbol, and $\sqrt{2}$ is it. Square roots
undo squaring, and they're the first numbers you'll meet that aren't
rational - which will matter next topic.

## The Big Idea

$x^2 = 9$ has two solutions, $3$ and $-3$. Both square to $9$. The
*arithmetic* square root picks the non-negative one: $\sqrt{9} = 3$,
never $-3$. That's a convention, but a necessary one - a symbol has to
mean one number - and it makes $\sqrt{a}$ the side of a square of
area $a$, which is what it was for.

Two things follow. You can't take the square root of a negative
number: no real number squares to $-4$, so $\sqrt{-4}$ is undefined.
And $\sqrt{a}$ is itself never negative. On the parabola $y = x^2$,
$\sqrt{a}$ is the *right-hand* $x$ at height $a$.

## The Rule

The **arithmetic square root** of a non-negative number $a$ is the
non-negative number whose square is $a$:

$$
\sqrt{a} = b \quad \text{means} \quad b \geq 0 \ \text{ and } \ b^2 = a
$$

Under the root sign, $a \geq 0$ (the **radicand**). And $\sqrt{a} \geq 0$
always.

$$
\sqrt{0} = 0, \quad \sqrt{1} = 1, \quad \sqrt{4} = 2, \quad \sqrt{9} = 3, \quad \sqrt{0.25} = 0.5, \quad \sqrt{\tfrac{4}{9}} = \tfrac{2}{3}
$$

Roots of numbers that aren't perfect squares - $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$,
$\sqrt{10}$ - are not rational; they're written with the root sign and
approximated when needed: $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$.

## Worked Example

Find $\sqrt{49}$, $\sqrt{0.09}$, $\sqrt{\frac{25}{16}}$, $\sqrt{1.44}$; decide
whether $\sqrt{-9}$ and $-\sqrt{9}$ make sense; and estimate $\sqrt{30}$
to one decimal place.

- **$\sqrt{49} = 7$** since $7 \geq 0$ and $7^2 = 49$.
- **$\sqrt{0.09} = 0.3$**, **$\sqrt{\frac{25}{16}} = \frac{5}{4}$**, **$\sqrt{1.44}
  = 1.2$**.
- **$\sqrt{-9}$:** undefined - no number squares to $-9$.
- **$-\sqrt{9}$:** fine - it's $-3$, the opposite of a root, not the root of
  a negative.
- **$\sqrt{30}$:** $5^2 = 25 < 30 < 36 = 6^2$, so between $5$ and $6$.
  $5.5^2 = 30.25$ - just over; $5.4^2 = 29.16$ - under. So $\sqrt{30} \approx
  5.5$ (to one decimal place, $5.5$ since $30$ is closer to $30.25$).

The square: side $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$ m.

## Common Mistake

Writing $\sqrt{9} = \pm 3$. The equation $x^2 = 9$ has two roots, but
the *symbol* $\sqrt{9}$ names only the non-negative one, $3$. The two
solutions of $x^2 = 9$ are written $\sqrt{9}$ and $-\sqrt{9}$. The
opposite confusion: thinking $\sqrt{-9} = -3$ - but $(-3)^2 = 9$, not
$-9$; there's no square root of a negative number.

## The Takeaway

$\sqrt{a}$ is the one non-negative number whose square is $a$; it
exists only for $a \geq 0$ and is never negative. Perfect squares have
rational roots; the rest are new numbers, written with the root sign
and estimated by squeezing between squares.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $\sqrt{64}$, $\sqrt{0.16}$ and $\sqrt{\frac{1}{100}}$.
2. Find $\sqrt{121} - \sqrt{81}$.
3. Which make sense: $\sqrt{-16}$, $-\sqrt{16}$, $\sqrt{0}$?
4. Between which two consecutive integers is $\sqrt{50}$?
5. Solve $x^2 = 16$ and write the roots using the root sign.
6. Someone writes $\sqrt{25} = \pm 5$. What's the correct value of the
   symbol?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $8$; $0.4$; $0.1$.
> 2. $11 - 9 = 2$.
> 3. $-\sqrt{16} = -4$ and $\sqrt{0} = 0$; $\sqrt{-16}$ is undefined.
> 4. $7$ and $8$.
> 5. $x = \sqrt{16} = 4$ or $x = -\sqrt{16} = -4$.
> 6. $5$ - the arithmetic root is the non-negative one.
