## Why It Matters

A loaf costs $32.50$ and milk costs $41.75$ - what's the total? You give
$100$ - what's the change? A jump of $6.85$ m against a record of $7.1$ -
how far short? Money, distances, weights, times: almost every number you
add up outside a maths lesson has a decimal point in it. And adding them
is column addition with one extra rule about where to put the points.

## The Big Idea

Column addition works because it lines up jars: ones under ones, tens
under tens. With decimals, the jars continue to the right of the point,
and the same rule holds - tenths must sit under tenths, hundredths under
hundredths. The point is the landmark that makes that happen: line up
the points and every jar is automatically under its twin.

After that, nothing is new. Add each column from the right, carry when
a column reaches ten, and put the point in the answer straight below the
points above it. If one number has fewer decimal places, imagine zeros
in the empty jars - $32.5$ is $32.50$.

## The Rule

To add or subtract decimals in a column:

1. **Write the numbers so that the decimal points are exactly one under
   the other** - then tenths are under tenths, hundredths under
   hundredths.
2. **Pad the shorter fractional part with zeros** so both have the same
   number of decimal places.
3. **Add or subtract as with natural numbers**, from the right, carrying
   or borrowing as usual.
4. **Put the decimal point in the answer directly under the points
   above.**

$$
32.50 + 41.75 = 74.25, \qquad 100.00 - 74.25 = 25.75
$$

The properties of addition still hold - terms can be swapped and
regrouped - and subtraction is still checked by adding back.

## Worked Example

Find $7.1 - 6.85$.

- **Line up the points:** $7.1$ over $6.85$, points in one column.
- **Pad:** $7.1$ becomes $7.10$.
- **Subtract from the right.** Hundredths: $0 - 5$ - can't, borrow from
  the tenths: $10 - 5 = 5$, and the tenths go from $1$ to $0$. Tenths:
  $0 - 8$ - can't, borrow from the ones: $10 - 8 = 2$, and the ones go
  from $7$ to $6$. Ones: $6 - 6 = 0$.
- **Point goes under the points:** $0.25$.

So the jump was $0.25$ m short. Check: $6.85 + 0.25 = 7.10$.

Now the shopping: $32.50 + 41.75$. Hundredths $0 + 5 = 5$; tenths
$5 + 7 = 12$, write $2$ carry $1$; ones $2 + 1 + 1 = 4$; tens $3 + 4 = 7$.
Total $74.25$. Change from $100$: $100.00 - 74.25 = 25.75$.

## Common Mistake

Lining up the last digits instead of the points. Write $7.1$ over $6.85$
with the $1$ above the $5$, and you're adding tenths to hundredths - the
result, $6.14$ or thereabouts, is nonsense. The rightmost digits of two
decimals are usually in *different* places; the point is the only thing
that's always in the same one. Line up the points, pad with zeros, and
the columns take care of themselves.

## The Takeaway

Adding and subtracting decimals is column arithmetic with the decimal
points in one vertical line. Pad with zeros so the fractional parts are
the same length, work from the right as usual, and drop the point into
the answer directly below.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $4.68 + 3.5$.
2. Find $12.4 - 5.75$.
3. Find $0.9 + 0.35 + 2.05$.
4. Find $10 - 3.62$.
5. A runner's first lap took $58.4$ s and the second $61.75$ s. What was
   the total time?
6. Someone adds $7.1 + 6.85$ by lining up the $1$ under the $5$ and gets
   $7.56$. What's the right answer?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $8.18$.
> 2. $6.65$.
> 3. $3.3$ (or $3.30$).
> 4. $6.38$.
> 5. $120.15$ s.
> 6. $13.95$ - the points must be aligned: $7.10 + 6.85$.
