## Why It Matters

$3x - 7 + 5x + 2 - x$ is a mouthful. It's also just $7x - 5$. Every
equation you'll solve starts with tidying an expression down to as few
terms as it can have, and that tidying is one move: gather the terms
that are alike and add their coefficients. Three apples and five apples
are eight apples; three $x$'s and five $x$'s are eight $x$'s.

## The Big Idea

$3x + 5x$ is three $x$'s plus five $x$'s - eight $x$'s. Why? The
distributive property, run backwards: $3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x$. The
letter part is a common factor; pull it out, add what's left. That
only works when the letter parts are *identical* - $3x$ and $5x$, or
$2ab$ and $-7ab$. It doesn't work for $3x + 5y$, because there's no
common factor to pull out: three apples and five pears stay three
apples and five pears.

Terms with the same letter part are called like terms, and adding
their coefficients is collecting them.

## The Rule

**Like terms** are terms with the same letter part (or no letter part
at all): $3x$ and $-5x$; $2ab$ and $ab$; $7$ and $-4$. They may differ only
in their coefficients.

**To collect like terms**, add their coefficients and keep the letter
part:

$$
3x + 5x - x = (3 + 5 - 1)x = 7x
$$

A general plan for simplifying an expression:

1. expand any brackets;
2. underline or mark the like terms - each group its own way;
3. add the coefficients within each group;
4. write the result, usually letters first, plain numbers last.

Terms that aren't like anything stay as they are.

## Worked Example

Simplify $3x - 7 + 5x + 2 - x$ and $4(a - 2b) - 3(2a - b) + 5$.

- **First expression.** Like terms with $x$: $3x$, $5x$, $-x$;
  coefficients $3 + 5 - 1 = 7$, so $7x$. Plain numbers: $-7 + 2 = -5$.
  Result: $7x - 5$.
- **Second expression.** Expand: $4a - 8b - 6a + 3b + 5$. Terms with $a$:
  $4a - 6a = -2a$. Terms with $b$: $-8b + 3b = -5b$. Number: $5$. Result:
  $-2a - 5b + 5$.

Check with a value: put $x = 2$ into the first. Original: $6 - 7 + 10 + 2 -
2 = 9$. Simplified: $14 - 5 = 9$. Same.

## Common Mistake

Collecting terms that aren't alike. $3x + 5y$ is not $8xy$, and $2a + 3$
is not $5a$ - the letter parts differ, so nothing can be pulled out.
And the sign travels with the coefficient: in $3x - 7 + 5x + 2 - x$, the
last term is $-x$ with coefficient $-1$, not $+1$. Mark each term with
its sign before you gather them.

## The Takeaway

Like terms share a letter part; collect them by adding coefficients -
signs included - and keeping the letters. Expand brackets first, sort
into groups, add each group. Unlike terms simply stay.

## Check Yourself

1. Simplify $8m - 3m + m$.
2. Simplify $5a + 2b - 7a + 4b$.
3. Simplify $2x - 9 - 6x + 4$.
4. Simplify $-3(p - 2) + 5p - 1$.
5. Simplify $2(3c - d) - (c - 4d)$.
6. Someone simplifies $4x + 3y$ to $7xy$. Why is that wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $6m$.
> 2. $-2a + 6b$.
> 3. $-4x - 5$.
> 4. $-3p + 6 + 5p - 1 = 2p + 5$.
> 5. $6c - 2d - c + 4d = 5c + 2d$.
> 6. $4x$ and $3y$ have different letter parts; they aren't like terms and
>    can't be combined.
