## Why It Matters

$0.25 \cdot 37 \cdot 4$ looks like a job for pen and paper. It's not:
$0.25 \cdot 4 = 1$, so the answer is $37$ - no column needed. $1.6 \cdot
100$, $4.8 \cdot 0.5$, $3.7 \cdot 0.1$ - every one of these has a shortcut,
and the shortcuts are the same three properties you used for natural
numbers, plus a couple of facts about the decimal point. Knowing them is
the difference between calculating and just seeing the answer.

## The Big Idea

The properties of multiplication - commutative, associative,
distributive - never cared what kind of numbers were involved. They were
about rearranging a rectangle of dots, and a rectangle $2.5$ wide by $4$
tall is still the same rectangle turned on its side. So every trick from
natural numbers still works: pair up factors that make a round number,
split an awkward factor into a sum, pull a shared factor out.

What's new is the point. Multiplying by $10$ moves every digit one jar
to the left - ten times bigger - which looks like the point sliding one
place right. Multiplying by $0.1$, which is a tenth, does the opposite:
one jar to the right, the point sliding left. Multiplying by a number
less than $1$ makes things *smaller*, and that's not a mistake - taking a
tenth of something should.

## The Rule

For any numbers $a$, $b$, $c$ - decimals included:

- $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$ (commutative);
- $(a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$ (associative);
- $a \cdot (b + c) = a \cdot b + a \cdot c$ and $a \cdot (b - c) = a \cdot
  b - a \cdot c$ (distributive).

Special cases:

- **By $10$, $100$, $1000$:** move the point right by $1$, $2$, $3$
  places: $1.6 \cdot 100 = 160$.
- **By $0.1$, $0.01$, $0.001$:** move the point left by $1$, $2$, $3$
  places: $37 \cdot 0.01 = 0.37$.
- **By $0.5$:** halve the number, since $0.5 = \frac{1}{2}$: $4.8 \cdot 0.5
  = 2.4$. Likewise $0.25$ is a quarter.
- **By $1$ and $0$:** $a \cdot 1 = a$, $a \cdot 0 = 0$, as always.

Pairs worth memorising: $0.25 \cdot 4 = 1$, $0.5 \cdot 2 = 1$, $2.5 \cdot 4
= 10$, $1.25 \cdot 8 = 10$, $0.125 \cdot 8 = 1$.

## Worked Example

Find $2.5 \cdot 7.3 \cdot 4$ in the easiest way.

- **Spot the pair.** $2.5 \cdot 4 = 10$.
- **Rearrange:** by the commutative and associative properties, $2.5
  \cdot 7.3 \cdot 4 = (2.5 \cdot 4) \cdot 7.3$.
- **Multiply:** $10 \cdot 7.3 = 73$.

Now the distributive property: $6.4 \cdot 9.9$. Write $9.9 = 10 - 0.1$;
then $6.4 \cdot 10 - 6.4 \cdot 0.1 = 64 - 0.64 = 63.36$.

And in reverse: $3.7 \cdot 8.2 + 3.7 \cdot 1.8$. The shared factor is
$3.7$: $3.7 \cdot (8.2 + 1.8) = 3.7 \cdot 10 = 37$.

## Common Mistake

Moving the point the wrong way. Multiplying by $0.1$ makes a number
*smaller*, so the point goes *left*: $37 \cdot 0.1 = 3.7$, not $370$. The
check is the meaning - $0.1$ is a tenth, and a tenth of $37$ can't be
bigger than $37$. Multiplying by a number bigger than $1$ enlarges; by a
number between $0$ and $1$ shrinks.

## The Takeaway

Swap, regroup and distribute decimals exactly as you did natural numbers,
hunting for pairs that make $1$, $10$ or $100$. A factor of $10$, $100$,
$1000$ slides the point right; a factor of $0.1$, $0.01$, $0.001$ slides
it left; a factor less than $1$ always makes the number smaller.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $0.25 \cdot 19 \cdot 4$.
2. Find $5.83 \cdot 1000$ and $5.83 \cdot 0.001$.
3. Find $4.6 \cdot 0.5$ and $4.6 \cdot 0.25$.
4. Find $7.2 \cdot 10.1$ using the distributive property.
5. Find $2.9 \cdot 4.6 + 2.9 \cdot 5.4$.
6. Someone finds $4.5 \cdot 0.01 = 450$. What's wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $(0.25 \cdot 4) \cdot 19 = 19$.
> 2. $5830$ and $0.00583$.
> 3. $2.3$ and $1.15$.
> 4. $7.2 \cdot 10 + 7.2 \cdot 0.1 = 72 + 0.72 = 72.72$.
> 5. $2.9 \cdot (4.6 + 5.4) = 2.9 \cdot 10 = 29$.
> 6. Multiplying by $0.01$ shrinks the number: the point moves two places
>    left, $0.045$.
