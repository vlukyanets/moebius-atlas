## Why It Matters

"A father is $28$ years older than his son, and in $4$ years he'll be
three times as old. How old is the son?" Try guessing - it takes a
while. Write it as an equation and it takes three lines. Word problems
are how maths meets the real world, and equations are how you stop
guessing: name the unknown, translate the story into symbols, solve.

## The Big Idea

Every word problem hides an equality - two descriptions of the same
thing that must be equal. "In $4$ years the father will be three times as
old" is one: the father's age then, described two ways. Call the son's
age $x$; then the father is $x + 28$; in four years they're $x + 4$ and $x +
32$; and the story says $x + 32 = 3(x + 4)$. Everything after that is
the last topic.

The skill is the translation. Choose the unknown well - usually the
smallest quantity, or the one the question asks for - and write every
other quantity in terms of it before you look for the equality.

## The Rule

To solve a problem with an equation:

1. **Choose the unknown** and give it a letter - say what it stands for,
   with units.
2. **Express the other quantities** through the unknown, following the
   story.
3. **Find the equality** the problem states - two expressions for the
   same thing - and write it as an equation.
4. **Solve** the equation.
5. **Check** the root against the story, and **answer the question
   asked** - which may not be the unknown itself.

A root that makes no sense in the story - a negative number of people,
half a person - means the equation was set up wrong.

## Worked Example

A father is $28$ years older than his son. In $4$ years he'll be three
times as old as the son. How old is the son now?

- **Unknown:** the son's age now, $x$ years.
- **Other quantities:** father now $x + 28$; in $4$ years, son $x + 4$,
  father $x + 32$.
- **Equality:** father's age then $=$ three times son's age then:
  $x + 32 = 3(x + 4)$.
- **Solve:** $x + 32 = 3x + 12$; $32 - 12 = 3x - x$; $20 = 2x$; $x = 10$.
- **Check:** son $10$, father $38$; in $4$ years, $14$ and $42$, and $42 = 3
  \cdot 14$. Correct.

The son is $10$.

Another: two shelves hold $60$ books; moving $8$ from the first to the
second makes them equal. How many were on each? Let the first hold $x$;
the second holds $60 - x$. After the move: $x - 8 = 60 - x + 8$, so $2x
= 76$, $x = 38$. First $38$, second $22$.

## Common Mistake

Writing the equation before defining the unknown, or answering with the
unknown when the question asked for something else. "$x = 10$" isn't an
answer until you say $x$ is the son's age - and if the question had
been the *father's* age, $10$ would be wrong; it's $38$. Name the letter
first, read the question again last.

## The Takeaway

Name the unknown, write everything else through it, find the two
descriptions of one quantity that the story makes equal, solve, and
check against the story. Then answer the actual question.

## Check Yourself

1. A number increased by $17$ equals three times the number. Find it.
2. Two numbers differ by $12$ and add up to $40$. Find them.
3. A rectangle is $5$ cm longer than it is wide, with perimeter $38$ cm.
   Find its sides.
4. Three friends collected $90$ stamps; the second has twice as many as
   the first, the third $10$ more than the first. How many has each?
5. A mother is $24$ years older than her daughter; in $6$ years she'll be
   three times as old. How old is the mother now?
6. Someone solves the father-and-son problem, gets $x = 10$, and answers
   "the father is $10$." What went wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $x + 17 = 3x$, $x = 8.5$.
> 2. $x + (x + 12) = 40$, $x = 14$: $14$ and $26$.
> 3. $2(x + x + 5) = 38$, $x = 7$: $7$ cm and $12$ cm.
> 4. $x + 2x + (x + 10) = 90$, $x = 20$: $20$, $40$, $30$.
> 5. $x + 30 = 3(x + 6)$, $x = 6$; the mother is $30$.
> 6. $x$ was the son's age; the father is $x + 28 = 38$.
