## Why It Matters

If $3$ notebooks cost $45$ hryvnias, what do $7$ cost? If a car does
$120$ km on $8$ litres, how far on $14$? If a recipe for $4$ people needs
$300$ g of flour, how much for $6$? Every one of these is two equal
ratios with one number missing, and there's one rule that finds it -
the same rule every time, whatever the story.

## The Big Idea

"Three notebooks to $45$ hryvnias" and "seven notebooks to $x$ hryvnias"
are the same ratio, because the price per notebook doesn't change. Two
equal ratios written with an equals sign between them is a proportion:
$3 : 45 = 7 : x$.

The useful fact is what happens when you multiply across. Write the
proportion as fractions, $\frac{3}{45} = \frac{7}{x}$, and multiply both
sides by $45x$: $3x = 45 \cdot 7$. The outer numbers multiplied equal the
inner numbers multiplied. That turns any proportion into a one-line
equation - and finding $x$ is dividing.

## The Rule

A **proportion** is an equality of two ratios:

$$
a : b = c : d \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}
$$

Here $a$ and $d$ are the **extremes** (outer terms) and $b$ and $c$ are
the **means** (inner terms).

**Basic property of a proportion:** in a true proportion, the product
of the extremes equals the product of the means:

$$
a \cdot d = b \cdot c
$$

And conversely - if $ad = bc$ for non-zero numbers, then $a : b = c : d$.

To find an unknown term, divide the product of the other pair by the
term's partner:

$$
x = \frac{b \cdot c}{d} \text{ if } x \text{ is an extreme}, \qquad
x = \frac{a \cdot d}{c} \text{ if } x \text{ is a mean}
$$

## Worked Example

A car does $120$ km on $8$ litres of petrol. How far does it go on $14$
litres?

- **Write the proportion:** kilometres to litres stays the same, so
  $120 : 8 = x : 14$.
- **Cross-multiply:** extremes $120 \cdot 14$, means $8 \cdot x$. So
  $8x = 120 \cdot 14 = 1680$.
- **Solve:** $x = 1680 : 8 = 210$.

The car goes $210$ km. Check: $120 : 8 = 15$ and $210 : 14 = 15$ - both
ratios are $15$ km per litre.

Is $4 : 6 = 10 : 15$ a true proportion? Extremes $4 \cdot 15 = 60$, means
$6 \cdot 10 = 60$. Equal - yes.

## Common Mistake

Writing the two ratios in different orders. "$120$ km to $8$ litres" on
the left and "$14$ litres to $x$ km" on the right gives $120 : 8 = 14 :
x$, and cross-multiplying that yields nonsense. Both ratios must
compare the same kind of thing in the same position: kilometres first
in both, litres second in both. Label the terms before you write them.

## The Takeaway

A proportion is two equal ratios; in a true one the product of the
extremes equals the product of the means. Cross-multiply to check a
proportion or to find its missing term - and keep the same order in
both ratios.

## Check Yourself

1. Is $3 : 5 = 12 : 20$ a true proportion? Check by the basic property.
2. Find $x$ in $x : 9 = 4 : 6$.
3. Find $x$ in $2.5 : x = 5 : 8$.
4. $3$ notebooks cost $45$ hryvnias. What do $7$ cost?
5. A recipe for $4$ people needs $300$ g of flour. How much for $6$?
6. Someone writes $120 : 8 = 14 : x$ for the petrol problem and gets
   $x \approx 0.93$. What went wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $3 \cdot 20 = 60 = 5 \cdot 12$ - true.
> 2. $x = 9 \cdot 4 : 6 = 6$.
> 3. $x = 2.5 \cdot 8 : 5 = 4$.
> 4. $3 : 45 = 7 : x$, $x = 105$ hryvnias.
> 5. $4 : 300 = 6 : x$, $x = 450$ g.
> 6. The second ratio was written litres-first; it should be $120 : 8 =
>    x : 14$, giving $210$ km.
