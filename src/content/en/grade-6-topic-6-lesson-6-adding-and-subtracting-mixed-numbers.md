## Why It Matters

A hike is $3\frac{1}{2}$ km to the lake and $2\frac{3}{4}$ km on to the
hut. A plank of $5\frac{1}{3}$ m has $1\frac{5}{6}$ m sawn off. In grade 5
you added mixed numbers whose fractions matched; now the fractional
parts can have any denominators - and the recipe is the same one, with
one extra step you already know.

## The Big Idea

A mixed number is wholes plus a fraction, so adding two of them is
wholes with wholes and fractions with fractions - as before. The only
new thing is that the fractions may need a common denominator before
they'll add. Halves and quarters become quarters, thirds and sixths
become sixths, and from there it's grade 5: carry a whole up if the
fraction overflows, borrow a whole down if the top fraction is too small
to subtract from.

## The Rule

**To add mixed numbers:** bring the fractional parts to a common
denominator, add the whole parts and the fractional parts separately,
and carry if the fractional part is improper.

$$
3\frac{1}{2} + 2\frac{3}{4} = 3\frac{2}{4} + 2\frac{3}{4} = 5\frac{5}{4} = 6\frac{1}{4}
$$

**To subtract mixed numbers:** bring the fractional parts to a common
denominator; if the top fraction is smaller, borrow $1$ from its whole
part as "denominator over denominator"; then subtract wholes and
fractions separately.

$$
5\frac{1}{3} - 1\frac{5}{6} = 5\frac{2}{6} - 1\frac{5}{6} = 4\frac{8}{6} - 1\frac{5}{6} = 3\frac{3}{6} = 3\frac{1}{2}
$$

Converting both to improper fractions always works too, and is often
easier for subtraction.

## Worked Example

Find $4\frac{2}{5} - 1\frac{3}{4}$.

- **Common denominator** of $5$ and $4$: $20$. So $4\frac{8}{20} -
  1\frac{15}{20}$.
- **Top fraction too small** ($8 < 15$): borrow. $4\frac{8}{20} = 3 +
  \frac{20}{20} + \frac{8}{20} = 3\frac{28}{20}$.
- **Subtract:** wholes $3 - 1 = 2$, fractions $\frac{28}{20} - \frac{15}{20}
  = \frac{13}{20}$.

So $4\frac{2}{5} - 1\frac{3}{4} = 2\frac{13}{20}$. Check: $2\frac{13}{20} +
1\frac{15}{20} = 3\frac{28}{20} = 4\frac{8}{20} = 4\frac{2}{5}$.

The hike: $3\frac{1}{2} + 2\frac{3}{4} = 6\frac{1}{4}$ km. The plank:
$5\frac{1}{3} - 1\frac{5}{6} = 3\frac{1}{2}$ m.

## Common Mistake

Forgetting to convert the fractions before adding or subtracting them.
$3\frac{1}{2} + 2\frac{3}{4}$ is not $5\frac{4}{6}$ - halves and quarters
were added as if they were the same size. Bring the fractional parts to
one denominator first, exactly as with plain fractions; the whole parts
never needed converting, which is what makes this easy to overlook.

## The Takeaway

Wholes with wholes, fractions with fractions - after the fractions have
a common denominator. Carry a whole up when the fraction overflows,
borrow one down when it's too small to subtract from.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $2\frac{1}{3} + 3\frac{1}{4}$.
2. Find $1\frac{5}{6} + 2\frac{2}{3}$.
3. Find $6\frac{3}{4} - 2\frac{1}{8}$.
4. Find $5\frac{1}{4} - 2\frac{2}{3}$.
5. Two bags weigh $2\frac{3}{5}$ kg and $1\frac{1}{2}$ kg. What's the
   difference, and what's the total?
6. Someone finds $3\frac{1}{2} + 2\frac{3}{4} = 5\frac{4}{6}$. What went
   wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $5\frac{7}{12}$.
> 2. $3\frac{9}{6} = 4\frac{1}{2}$.
> 3. $4\frac{5}{8}$.
> 4. $5\frac{3}{12} - 2\frac{8}{12} = 4\frac{15}{12} - 2\frac{8}{12} =
>    2\frac{7}{12}$.
> 5. Difference $1\frac{1}{10}$ kg, total $4\frac{1}{10}$ kg.
> 6. The fractions weren't brought to a common denominator; the answer is
>    $6\frac{1}{4}$.
