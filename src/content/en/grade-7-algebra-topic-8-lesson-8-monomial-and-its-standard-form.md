## Why It Matters

$3 \cdot a \cdot b \cdot 2 \cdot a \cdot b \cdot b$ and $6a^2b^3$ are the
same thing, but only one of them can be read at a glance, compared
with another, or added to a like term. Algebra is built from
expressions like these - products of numbers and variables - and it
insists they be written in one standard way. Get that habit now and
every polynomial from here on is tidy.

## The Big Idea

A monomial is the simplest kind of expression: only multiplication,
no adding or subtracting. Numbers, variables, powers of variables,
multiplied together - that's all. And since multiplication can be
reordered and regrouped freely, any such product can be tidied: the
numbers gathered into one coefficient at the front, each variable's
copies gathered into one power, the variables in alphabetical order.
That tidy version is the standard form, and it's unique - two
monomials are equal exactly when their standard forms match.

The degree of a monomial counts how many variable factors it has in
all: $6a^2b^3$ has two $a$'s and three $b$'s, degree $5$.

## The Rule

A **monomial** is a product of numbers, variables and powers of
variables. A single number or a single variable is also a monomial.
($a + b$ and $\frac{2}{x}$ are not.)

The **standard form** of a monomial is the product of one numerical
factor - the **coefficient** - written first, and powers of *different*
variables, each variable appearing once:

$$
3 \cdot a \cdot b \cdot 2 \cdot a \cdot b \cdot b = 6a^2b^3
$$

The **degree** of a monomial is the sum of the exponents of all its
variables. A non-zero number has degree $0$.

To bring a monomial to standard form: multiply the numbers, then
combine each variable's powers by adding exponents.

## Worked Example

Write $-2x \cdot 5x^3y \cdot y^2$ in standard form, give its coefficient
and degree; then decide which of $7$, $\frac{a}{3}$, $\frac{3}{a}$, $2x - y$,
$-m^2n$ are monomials.

- **Numbers:** $-2 \cdot 5 = -10$. **$x$'s:** $x \cdot x^3 = x^4$. **$y$'s:**
  $y \cdot y^2 = y^3$. Standard form: $-10x^4y^3$.
- **Coefficient:** $-10$. **Degree:** $4 + 3 = 7$.
- **$7$:** a monomial (a number), degree $0$. **$\frac{a}{3}$:** yes - it's
  $\frac{1}{3}a$, coefficient $\frac{1}{3}$. **$\frac{3}{a}$:** no -
  division by a variable. **$2x - y$:** no - a subtraction. **$-m^2n$:**
  yes, coefficient $-1$, degree $3$.

Also: $(3ab^2)^2 = 9a^2b^4$ is a monomial of degree $6$; and $0.5x \cdot
4x = 2x^2$.

## Common Mistake

Adding exponents of *different* variables, or multiplying coefficients
into exponents. $x^4y^3$ cannot become $xy^7$ - the $x$'s and $y$'s are
separate counts. And $-2x \cdot 5x^3$ is $-10x^4$, not $-10x^3$ or
$-7x^4$: coefficients multiply, exponents of the same variable add.
Keep the two bookkeeping columns apart.

## The Takeaway

A monomial is a product of numbers and variable powers; its standard
form has one coefficient in front and each variable once, in order.
The degree is the total exponent count. Standard form is what makes
monomials comparable and addable.

## Check Yourself

1. Write $4a \cdot a^2 \cdot (-3b)$ in standard form.
2. Give the coefficient and degree of $-\frac{2}{5}x^3y^2z$.
3. Which are monomials: $5m$, $m + 5$, $\frac{m}{5}$, $\frac{5}{m}$, $m^5$?
4. Write $(2xy^2)^3$ in standard form and give its degree.
5. Find the value of $-3a^2b$ for $a = 2$, $b = -1$.
6. Someone writes $2x^2 \cdot 3x^3 = 6x^6$. What's the right form?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $-12a^3b$.
> 2. $-\frac{2}{5}$; $6$.
> 3. $5m$, $\frac{m}{5}$, $m^5$.
> 4. $8x^3y^6$; degree $9$.
> 5. $-3 \cdot 4 \cdot (-1) = 12$.
> 6. $6x^5$ - exponents of the same variable add.
