## Why It Matters

$-3 \cdot (x - 5)$ is stuck as it is - you can't do anything with a
bracket that has a letter inside. Multiply it out and it becomes
$-3x + 15$, which can be combined with other terms, moved across an
equation, simplified. Every equation from here on, and all of algebra,
runs on opening and closing brackets like this - and with negatives in
the mix, the signs are the whole game.

## The Big Idea

The distributive property said a number times a sum is the sum of the
separate products: $a(b + c) = ab + ac$. It's still true with negatives,
for the same reason - and it swallows the bracket-expanding rule from
a few topics ago. A minus in front of a bracket is a factor of $-1$:
$-(b - c) = (-1)(b - c) = -b + c$. Multiplying by $-3$ does the same thing
three times over: $-3(x - 5) = -3x + 15$. The sign of each product
follows the multiplication rule, term by term.

Run backwards, the property takes a common factor *out* of a sum:
$-3x + 15 = -3(x - 5)$. Going in is expanding; going out is factoring.

## The Rule

For any rational numbers $a$, $b$, $c$:

$$
a(b + c) = ab + ac, \qquad a(b - c) = ab - ac
$$

To multiply a bracket by a number, multiply *every* term inside by it,
signs included, and write the results with their resulting signs.

In reverse, a common factor can be taken out of the brackets:

$$
ab + ac = a(b + c)
$$

Also: $-1 \cdot (b + c) = -b - c$, which is the bracket rule.

## Worked Example

Expand $-3(x - 5)$ and $-\frac{1}{2}(4a - 6b + 8)$, and factor
$-5m + 20$.

- **$-3(x - 5)$.** $-3 \cdot x = -3x$; $-3 \cdot (-5) = +15$. Result
  $-3x + 15$.
- **$-\frac{1}{2}(4a - 6b + 8)$.** $-\frac{1}{2} \cdot 4a = -2a$;
  $-\frac{1}{2} \cdot (-6b) = +3b$; $-\frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 = -4$. Result
  $-2a + 3b - 4$.
- **$-5m + 20$.** Both terms have a factor of $5$; taking out $-5$ gives
  $-5(m - 4)$. Check by expanding: $-5m + 20$.

And a numeric shortcut: $-7 \cdot 98 = -7(100 - 2) = -700 + 14 = -686$.

## Common Mistake

Multiplying only the first term. $-3(x - 5)$ is not $-3x - 5$: the $-3$
has to reach the $-5$ too, and $-3 \cdot (-5)$ is $+15$. Two errors hide
in the wrong answer - a term left unmultiplied and a sign left
unflipped. Draw arrows from the factor to every term inside the
bracket until it's automatic.

## The Takeaway

A number times a bracket multiplies every term inside, with the
multiplication sign rule applied to each. A minus outside is a factor
of $-1$. Run the other way, the same property pulls a common factor out
of a sum.

## Check Yourself

1. Expand $4(a - 3)$ and $-4(a - 3)$.
2. Expand $-2(3x + y - 1)$.
3. Expand $0.5(-6p + 4q)$.
4. Factor $6x - 18$ and $-7a - 7b$.
5. Find $-9 \cdot 101$ using the distributive property.
6. Someone expands $-2(x - 3)$ as $-2x - 6$. What's the right answer?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $4a - 12$ and $-4a + 12$.
> 2. $-6x - 2y + 2$.
> 3. $-3p + 2q$.
> 4. $6(x - 3)$ and $-7(a + b)$.
> 5. $-900 - 9 = -909$.
> 6. $-2x + 6$ - the $-2$ times $-3$ is $+6$.
