## Why It Matters

A thermometer laid on its side. A timeline with "BC" to the left of
year $0$ and "AD" to the right. The floor buttons in a lift with a car
park underneath. Every one of these is a number line: a straight line
where every number - positive, negative or zero - has exactly one
place. It's the picture behind every rule about negative numbers that's
coming, and once you can read it, those rules stop being rules and
start being obvious.

## The Big Idea

Take the coordinate ray from grade 5 - origin, direction, unit
segment - and let it keep going the other way past $0$. That's the whole
change. To the right, the marks are $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ as before. To the
left, they're $-1, -2, -3, \ldots$ - each the mirror image of its
positive twin across $0$. Every point now has a coordinate, and every
number has a point, including the ones that used to have nowhere to
go.

Distance from $0$ works the same on both sides: $-3$ is three units
from the origin, just like $3$. And moving right always means getting
bigger, moving left always means getting smaller - even down among the
negatives.

## The Rule

A **number line** (coordinate line) is a line with a chosen **origin**
$O$ (the point $0$), a **positive direction** marked by an arrow, and a
**unit segment**. Each point has a **coordinate**: positive to the right
of $O$, negative to the left, $0$ at $O$. The point with coordinate $a$
is written $A(a)$.

Points $a$ and $-a$ lie on opposite sides of the origin, at the same
distance from it.

The distance between two points with coordinates $a$ and $b$ is the
length of the segment between them; if $b$ is to the right of $a$, it's
$b - a$.

## Worked Example

Mark the points $A(3)$, $B(-2)$, $C(-4.5)$ and $D(0)$ on a number line,
and find the distance $AB$.

- **Draw the line:** origin, arrow to the right, unit segment one
  square.
- **Positive coordinates go right:** $A$ is $3$ squares right of $O$.
- **Negative go left:** $B$ is $2$ squares left of $O$; $C$ is $4$ and a
  half squares left.
- **$D$ is the origin itself.**
- **Distance $AB$:** from $-2$ to $0$ is $2$ units, from $0$ to $3$ is $3$
  more: $5$ units in all.

The timeline: a historian says an event was in $-44$ (44 BC) and another
in $14$ (14 AD). How many years apart? $44 + 14 = 58$ years - across the
origin, distances add.

## Common Mistake

Marking $-4.5$ *between* $-4$ and $-3$ "because $4.5$ is bigger than
$4$." On the left of zero, further from the origin means further left:
$-4.5$ lies between $-5$ and $-4$. The mirror image of $4.5$, which sits
between $4$ and $5$, sits between $-5$ and $-4$. Think of the left side as
the right side reflected, not as the right side continued.

## The Takeaway

The number line is the coordinate ray extended through zero: positives
to the right, negatives to the left, $a$ and $-a$ mirror images at the
same distance. Right is bigger, left is smaller, everywhere on the
line.

## Check Yourself

1. Which points lie to the left of the origin: $A(-1)$, $B(2)$, $C(-6)$,
   $D(0.5)$?
2. Which of $-3$ and $-7$ is further from the origin?
3. Mark $-1.5$: between which two integers does it lie?
4. Find the distance between $P(-3)$ and $Q(4)$.
5. Find the distance between $M(-8)$ and $N(-2)$.
6. Someone marks $-2.5$ between $-2$ and $-1$. Where should it be?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $A$ and $C$.
> 2. $-7$ - seven units away.
> 3. Between $-2$ and $-1$.
> 4. $3 + 4 = 7$.
> 5. $8 - 2 = 6$.
> 6. Between $-3$ and $-2$ - two and a half units left of zero.
