## Why It Matters

In grade 5 an equation was a puzzle with the unknown in one place, and
you undid one operation at a time. Now unknowns show up on both sides -
$5x - 7 = 2x + 8$ - and brackets, negatives and fractions join in. The
old trick doesn't stretch that far. Two properties do: they let you move
anything to either side and scale the whole equation, and with them
every equation in this course becomes the same short routine.

## The Big Idea

An equation is a balance. Whatever you do to one pan, do to the other
and it stays level: add the same weight to both, take the same weight
off both, double both, halve both. Those are the only moves - and they're
enough. Take $3x$ off both sides of $5x - 7 = 2x + 8$ and the $x$'s are
all on the left; add $7$ to both and the numbers are all on the right;
divide both by $3$ and $x$ is alone.

A shortcut falls out of the first move: subtracting $2x$ from both sides
looks exactly like the $2x$ walking across the equals sign and changing
its sign. That's "transposing," and it's the same thing.

## The Rule

An equation stays equivalent - same roots - under two operations:

1. **Adding the same number or expression to both sides** (or
   subtracting it). Consequence: **a term may be moved to the other
   side if its sign is changed.**
2. **Multiplying or dividing both sides by the same non-zero number.**

The standard routine for a linear equation:

1. expand brackets;
2. move terms with the unknown to one side and plain numbers to the
   other, changing signs as they cross;
3. collect like terms on each side, to get $ax = b$;
4. divide both sides by $a$: $x = b : a$.

Check by substituting the root into the original equation.

## Worked Example

Solve $5x - 7 = 2x + 8$ and $3(x - 2) = -x + 6$.

- **$5x - 7 = 2x + 8$.** Move $2x$ left (becomes $-2x$) and $-7$ right
  (becomes $+7$): $5x - 2x = 8 + 7$. Collect: $3x = 15$. Divide by $3$:
  $x = 5$. Check: $25 - 7 = 18$ and $10 + 8 = 18$.
- **$3(x - 2) = -x + 6$.** Expand: $3x - 6 = -x + 6$. Move: $3x + x = 6 +
  6$. Collect: $4x = 12$. Divide: $x = 3$. Check: $3 \cdot 1 = 3$ and
  $-3 + 6 = 3$.

With fractions: $\frac{x}{4} - 1 = \frac{x}{6}$. Multiply both sides by
$12$ to clear denominators: $3x - 12 = 2x$, so $x = 12$.

## Common Mistake

Moving a term across without changing its sign. From $5x - 7 = 2x + 8$
to $5x - 2x = 8 - 7$ - the $-7$ crossed and stayed $-7$. But crossing
*is* adding $7$ to both sides, and on the right that makes $+7$. Every
term that changes sides changes sign; terms that stay put keep theirs.
The check at the end catches this: $x = \frac{1}{3}$ from the wrong line
fails the substitution.

## The Takeaway

Two moves keep an equation true: add or subtract the same thing on both
sides - which is why a term can cross the equals sign with its sign
flipped - and multiply or divide both sides by the same non-zero
number. Unknowns left, numbers right, collect, divide, check.

## Check Yourself

1. Solve $4x + 9 = x - 6$.
2. Solve $7 - 2x = 3x + 22$.
3. Solve $2(3x - 1) = 5x + 4$.
4. Solve $-\frac{x}{3} = 4$.
5. Solve $\frac{x}{2} + \frac{x}{5} = 7$.
6. Someone solves $5x - 7 = 2x + 8$ by writing $5x - 2x = 8 - 7$. What
   went wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $3x = -15$, $x = -5$.
> 2. $-5x = 15$, $x = -3$.
> 3. $6x - 2 = 5x + 4$, $x = 6$.
> 4. $x = -12$.
> 5. Multiply by $10$: $5x + 2x = 70$, $x = 10$.
> 6. The $-7$ didn't change sign when it crossed; it should be $8 + 7$,
>    giving $x = 5$.
