## Why It Matters

Which is colder, $-3^\circ$ or $-7^\circ$? Which account is deeper in
debt, $-250$ or $-40$? Which is bigger, $-\frac{1}{2}$ or $-0.4$?
Comparing positive numbers is second nature by now; negatives seem to
turn the rules upside down - a bigger digit means a *smaller* number.
The number line settles it, and gives one rule that never fails.

## The Big Idea

On the number line, bigger is always to the right. That was true for
naturals and fractions, and nothing changes for negatives: $-3$ is to
the right of $-7$, so $-3 > -7$. The thermometer says the same thing -
$-3^\circ$ is warmer, higher up.

What *looks* upside down is that among negatives, the one with the
bigger absolute value is further left. $-7$ is seven units from zero,
$-3$ only three; the one that went further into the negatives is the
smaller number. So for two negatives, compare their sizes and flip the
answer.

## The Rule

Of two numbers on the number line, **the one further right is greater.**
From that:

- every positive number is greater than $0$, and every negative number
  is less than $0$;
- every positive number is greater than every negative number;
- of two negative numbers, the one with the **smaller absolute value**
  is greater:

$$
-3 > -7 \quad \text{because} \quad |-3| < |-7|
$$

Negative fractions and decimals are compared the same way: compare
their absolute values as you would positive fractions - common
denominator, or convert to decimals - and then reverse.

## Worked Example

Compare $-\frac{1}{2}$ and $-0.4$, and put $-2.5$, $0$, $-3$, $1.2$, $-0.7$
in ascending order.

- **$-\frac{1}{2}$ against $-0.4$.** Both negative, so compare absolute
  values: $\frac{1}{2} = 0.5$ and $0.4$; $0.5 > 0.4$. The bigger absolute
  value is the smaller number: $-\frac{1}{2} < -0.4$.
- **Ordering.** Negatives first, positives last, zero between. Among
  the negatives $-3$, $-2.5$, $-0.7$, absolute values $3 > 2.5 > 0.7$, so
  the order is $-3 < -2.5 < -0.7$. Then $0$, then $1.2$.
- **Result:** $-3 < -2.5 < -0.7 < 0 < 1.2$.

The debts: $-250 < -40$ - the $250$ account is deeper in debt, and that's
the smaller number.

## Common Mistake

Comparing negatives by their digits as if they were positive. "$-7 >
-3$ because $7 > 3$" - but $-7$ is further left, deeper in the cold, more
in debt. The digits tell how far from zero, and for negatives, further
from zero means *less*. Check against the thermometer: $-7^\circ$ is not
warmer than $-3^\circ$.

## The Takeaway

Right is greater on the number line, everywhere. Positives beat zero
beat negatives; and among negatives, the smaller absolute value is the
greater number - the one closer to zero.

## Check Yourself

1. Put in $<$ or $>$: $-5$ and $2$; $-5$ and $-2$; $0$ and $-0.1$.
2. Compare $-\frac{2}{3}$ and $-\frac{3}{4}$.
3. Compare $-1.05$ and $-1.5$.
4. Write in descending order: $-4$, $3$, $-0.5$, $0$, $-4.1$.
5. Which is colder: $-11^\circ$ or $-9^\circ$?
6. Someone says $-8 > -2$ "because $8 > 2$." Correct it.

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $-5 < 2$; $-5 < -2$; $0 > -0.1$.
> 2. $\frac{8}{12} < \frac{9}{12}$, so $-\frac{2}{3} > -\frac{3}{4}$.
> 3. $1.05 < 1.5$, so $-1.05 > -1.5$.
> 4. $3 > 0 > -0.5 > -4 > -4.1$.
> 5. $-11^\circ$.
> 6. $-8 < -2$: $-8$ is further from zero on the negative side, so it's
>    smaller.
