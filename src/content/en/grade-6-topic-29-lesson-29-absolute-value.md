## Why It Matters

A thermometer that read $-8^\circ$ this morning and $+8^\circ$ this
afternoon showed the same *amount* of temperature both times - eight
degrees away from zero, in different directions. A debt of $500$ and
savings of $500$ are the same *size*. Sometimes you need a number's
size without its sign - how far, how much, how big - and that's its
absolute value. It's the number the arithmetic of negatives is built
on.

## The Big Idea

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero on the number
line. Distance is never negative: $5$ is five units from the origin,
and so is $-5$. Strip the sign, keep the size. For a positive number
that changes nothing; for a negative one, it flips it to positive;
for zero, it's zero. Two vertical bars around a number mean "the
distance of this from zero."

## The Rule

The **absolute value** (modulus) of a number $a$, written $|a|$, is the
distance from the origin to the point $a$ on the number line:

$$
|a| = \begin{cases} a, & \text{if } a \geq 0 \\ -a, & \text{if } a < 0 \end{cases}
$$

$$
|7| = 7, \qquad |-7| = 7, \qquad |0| = 0
$$

Properties that follow:

- $|a| \geq 0$ always - it's a distance;
- opposite numbers have equal absolute values: $|a| = |-a|$;
- $|a| = 0$ only when $a = 0$;
- the distance between two points $a$ and $b$ on the number line is
  $|a - b|$.

## Worked Example

Find $|-4.5|$, $|3| - |-3|$ and $|-2| \cdot |-6|$, and solve $|x| = 5$.

- **$|-4.5| = 4.5$** - four and a half units from zero.
- **$|3| - |-3| = 3 - 3 = 0$** - both are $3$.
- **$|-2| \cdot |-6| = 2 \cdot 6 = 12$.**
- **$|x| = 5$:** which points are $5$ units from the origin? Two of them:
  $x = 5$ and $x = -5$.

And a comparison: which is bigger, $|-9|$ or $|4|$? $9 > 4$, so $|-9|$ -
even though $-9 < 4$ as numbers.

## Common Mistake

Thinking $|x| = 5$ has one solution, or that $|a|$ "removes the minus"
by rule and nothing else. Two points are five from zero, so $|x| = 5$
has two solutions. And $|a| = -a$ when $a$ is negative isn't a
contradiction: $-a$ is *positive* then - the opposite of a negative
number. Also, $|x| = -3$ has no solution at all: no distance is
negative.

## The Takeaway

The absolute value is the distance from zero - the size of a number
with its sign thrown away. It's never negative, opposites share it, and
$|x| = c$ for positive $c$ has two solutions, $c$ and $-c$.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $|-12|$, $|0.3|$, $|0|$.
2. Find $|-8| + |5|$ and $|-8| - |5|$.
3. Find $|-1.5| \cdot |-4|$.
4. Solve $|x| = 2.7$.
5. Does $|x| = -1$ have a solution? Why?
6. Someone says "$|a| = a$ for every number $a$." Give a number for which
   that's false.

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $12$; $0.3$; $0$.
> 2. $13$ and $3$.
> 3. $6$.
> 4. $x = 2.7$ or $x = -2.7$.
> 5. No - an absolute value is a distance and can't be negative.
> 6. Any negative number: $|-3| = 3 \neq -3$.
