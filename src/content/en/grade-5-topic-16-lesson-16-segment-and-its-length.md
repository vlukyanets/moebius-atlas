## Why It Matters

How long is the table, will the sofa fit through the door, how far is it
from your desk to the window? Before you can measure anything you need a
thing to measure - and the simplest one there is, a straight stretch
between two points, is a segment. Every shape you'll ever draw is made of
them.

## The Big Idea

Take two dots on a page and stretch a thread tight between them. The
thread is the segment, and the dots are its ends. Nothing about the
segment is loose: it starts at one end, stops at the other, and takes the
straightest possible route between them.

To measure it, you pick a unit - a centimetre, say - and count how many
times it fits end to end along the segment. That count is the length. It
doesn't depend on which end you start from, or on how the segment is
tilted on the page; it's a property of the segment itself.

## The Rule

A **segment** is the part of a straight line between two points, together
with those points. The points are its **ends**, and the segment with ends
$A$ and $B$ is written $AB$ (or $BA$ - same segment).

The **length** of a segment is how many units fit into it. The length of
$AB$ is written $AB$ too, so $AB = 5$ cm means "the segment $AB$ is $5$
centimetres long." Two segments are **equal** if their lengths are equal.

Length adds up: if the point $C$ lies on the segment $AB$, then

$$
AB = AC + CB
$$

The units of length step by tens and a thousand: $1$ cm $= 10$ mm,
$1$ dm $= 10$ cm, $1$ m $= 10$ dm $= 100$ cm, $1$ km $= 1000$ m.

A **broken line** is a chain of segments joined end to end; its length is
the sum of the lengths of its links.

## Worked Example

The point $C$ lies on the segment $AB$. $AC = 3$ cm $7$ mm and $CB = 5$ cm
$6$ mm. Find $AB$.

- **Same units first.** Turn everything into millimetres: $AC = 37$ mm,
  $CB = 56$ mm.
- **Add:** $AB = AC + CB = 37 + 56 = 93$ mm.
- **Back to a readable form:** $93$ mm $= 9$ cm $3$ mm.

So $AB = 9$ cm $3$ mm.

Now a broken line $ABCD$ with links $AB = 4$ cm, $BC = 25$ mm, $CD = 6$ cm.
Its length is $40 + 25 + 60 = 125$ mm $= 12$ cm $5$ mm.

## Common Mistake

Adding numbers in different units as if they were the same. $3$ cm $7$ mm
plus $5$ cm $6$ mm is not $8$ cm $13$ mm - thirteen millimetres is more
than a centimetre, so it should have been carried: $9$ cm $3$ mm. Either
convert everything to the smallest unit before adding, or remember that
$10$ mm makes a centimetre exactly the way $10$ ones make a ten.

## The Takeaway

A segment is a straight stretch between two points, and its length is the
number of units that fit along it. Lengths along one segment add up, and
they only add up properly in the same unit.

## Check Yourself

1. A point $M$ lies on $PQ$. $PM = 4$ cm, $MQ = 9$ cm. Find $PQ$.
2. $AB = 12$ cm and $C$ lies on $AB$ with $AC = 5$ cm. Find $CB$.
3. Express $2$ m $35$ cm in centimetres, and $4080$ m in kilometres and
   metres.
4. A broken line has links of $8$ cm, $45$ mm and $1$ dm. Find its length
   in millimetres.
5. Are the segments $AB = 3$ dm and $CD = 30$ cm equal?
6. Someone adds $6$ cm $8$ mm and $2$ cm $5$ mm and gets $8$ cm $13$ mm.
   What's the right answer?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $PQ = 4 + 9 = 13$ cm.
> 2. $CB = 12 - 5 = 7$ cm.
> 3. $235$ cm; $4$ km $80$ m.
> 4. $80 + 45 + 100 = 225$ mm.
> 5. Yes - $3$ dm is $30$ cm.
> 6. $9$ cm $3$ mm - the $13$ mm holds a whole centimetre.
