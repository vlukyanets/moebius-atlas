## Why It Matters

The area of a rectangle $3a$ by $4a^2$; the volume of a cube with edge
$2x$; the product that appears every time you expand brackets with
letters in them. Multiplying monomials is the atom of all polynomial
arithmetic, and it's nothing more than the properties of powers
applied to a product - done right, it always comes out in standard
form.

## The Big Idea

Two monomials multiplied together are just a longer product of
numbers and variables - so multiply, and tidy into standard form:
numbers with numbers, each variable's exponents added. $3a \cdot 4a^2 =
12a^3$. Raising a monomial to a power is the same idea one level up:
$(2x)^3$ is $2x \cdot 2x \cdot 2x = 8x^3$ - every factor inside gets the
exponent, and for a variable that already carries a power, exponents
multiply.

## The Rule

**To multiply monomials:** multiply the coefficients, and for each
variable add its exponents. The result is a monomial in standard
form.

$$
3a \cdot 4a^2 = 12a^3, \qquad (-2x^2y) \cdot (5xy^3) = -10x^3y^4
$$

**To raise a monomial to a power:** raise the coefficient to that
power, and multiply each variable's exponent by it.

$$
(2x)^3 = 8x^3, \qquad (-3a^2b)^2 = 9a^4b^2, \qquad (-a^3)^5 = -a^{15}
$$

Watch the sign of the coefficient: negative to an even power is
positive, to an odd power negative.

## Worked Example

Find $(-4m^2n) \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}mn^3\right)$, $(-2ab^2)^4$ and
$-(2ab^2)^4$; then the volume of a cube with edge $3x^2$.

- **Product.** Coefficients: $-4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = -2$. $m$: $2 + 1 = 3$.
  $n$: $1 + 3 = 4$. Result $-2m^3n^4$.
- **$(-2ab^2)^4$.** Coefficient $(-2)^4 = 16$. $a^{1 \cdot 4} = a^4$,
  $b^{2 \cdot 4} = b^8$. Result $16a^4b^8$.
- **$-(2ab^2)^4$.** The minus is outside: $-16a^4b^8$.
- **Cube.** $V = (3x^2)^3 = 27x^6$.

Combined: $(2a^2)^3 \cdot (-a)^2 = 8a^6 \cdot a^2 = 8a^8$.

## Common Mistake

Forgetting to raise the coefficient, or raising the exponent to the
power instead of multiplying. $(2x)^3$ is not $2x^3$ - the $2$ is cubed
too, $8x^3$. And $(a^2)^3$ is $a^6$, not $a^8$: exponents multiply. Write
the factors out once - $(2x)(2x)(2x)$ - if the rule ever feels
uncertain.

## The Takeaway

Multiply monomials by multiplying coefficients and adding each
variable's exponents. Raise a monomial to a power by raising the
coefficient and multiplying every exponent by the power - sign
included.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $5x^3 \cdot (-2x^4)$.
2. Find $(3ab) \cdot (2a^2b^3) \cdot (-b)$.
3. Find $(4x^2)^2$ and $(-x^2y)^3$.
4. Find $(-2a)^3 \cdot a^2$.
5. A rectangle is $2xy$ by $3x^2y$. Find its area.
6. Someone writes $(3a^2)^2 = 3a^4$. What's the right answer?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $-10x^7$.
> 2. $-6a^3b^5$.
> 3. $16x^4$; $-x^6y^3$.
> 4. $-8a^3 \cdot a^2 = -8a^5$.
> 5. $6x^3y^2$.
> 6. $9a^4$ - the coefficient is squared too.
