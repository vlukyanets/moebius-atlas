## Why It Matters

Open a door a crack, or fling it wide. Hands of a clock at three o'clock,
at six, at one. A ramp that's gentle or steep. In each case two straight
things meet at a point, and what matters is not how long they are but how
far apart they've swung. That "how far apart" is an angle - and it's the
second thing, after length, that geometry learns to measure.

## The Big Idea

Take two rays that start at the same point and think of them as the two
hands of a clock, pinned at the centre. At first they lie on top of each
other. Swing one of them round, and a gap opens between them - that gap
is the angle. Keep swinging: the gap grows, until the moving hand points
exactly the opposite way and the two rays make one straight line. That
straight angle is the biggest one that fits in this picture, and a
quarter-turn - the corner of a page - is the one everyone recognises: the
right angle.

An angle doesn't care how long its rays are drawn. Draw the same two rays
twice as long and the angle hasn't changed - it's the opening, not the
arms.

## The Rule

An **angle** is a figure made of two rays with a common origin. The
origin is the **vertex** of the angle, and the rays are its **sides**.
The angle with vertex $O$ and sides $OA$ and $OB$ is written $\angle AOB$
or $\angle BOA$ - the vertex letter always in the middle - or simply
$\angle O$ when no other angle shares that vertex.

Angles are sorted by how far open they are:

- A **straight angle** has its sides on one line, pointing opposite ways -
  the two sides are opposite rays.
- A **right angle** is half of a straight angle - the corner of a square.
  It's marked with a small square at the vertex.
- An **acute angle** is smaller than a right angle.
- An **obtuse angle** is bigger than a right angle but smaller than a
  straight one.

Two angles are **equal** if one can be laid over the other so that the
sides coincide.

A ray drawn from the vertex inside an angle splits it into two angles; a
ray that splits it into two equal angles is the **bisector**.

## Worked Example

The clock says exactly nine o'clock. Name the angle between the hands,
and say what kind it is.

- **Find the vertex and the sides.** Both hands start at the centre of the
  clock - that's the vertex. The hour hand points at $9$, the minute hand
  at $12$: those are the sides.
- **Compare with a quarter-turn.** From $12$ round to $9$ is three of the
  four quarters of the dial going one way - but the angle is the smaller
  opening, one quarter of the dial going the other way.
- **A quarter-turn is a right angle.** So the hands make a right angle.

At six o'clock the hands point opposite ways - a straight angle. At one
o'clock the gap is less than a quarter-turn - acute. At four o'clock it's
more than a quarter but less than a half - obtuse.

## Common Mistake

Judging an angle by the length of its sides. Two rays drawn $10$ cm long
look "bigger" than the same two rays drawn $2$ cm long, and some people
call the first angle larger. But the opening between the rays is
identical - the angles are equal. Look at how far apart the rays swing,
never at how far they're drawn.

## The Takeaway

An angle is two rays from one vertex, and its size is the opening between
them, not the length of the arms. A straight angle is a line, a right
angle is half of that, acute is less than a right angle and obtuse is
more.

## Check Yourself

1. Name the vertex and the sides of $\angle MKN$.
2. What kind of angle do the clock hands make at three o'clock? At six?
3. Can an obtuse angle be smaller than an acute one?
4. The ray $OC$ is the bisector of $\angle AOB$. What can you say about
   $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$?
5. How many right angles fit into a straight angle?
6. Someone says $\angle AOB$ with sides $5$ cm long is bigger than
   $\angle AOB$ with sides $2$ cm long. Where's the mistake?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. Vertex $K$, sides $KM$ and $KN$.
> 2. Right; straight.
> 3. No - every obtuse angle is bigger than a right angle, and every acute
>    one is smaller.
> 4. They're equal.
> 5. Two.
> 6. The length of the sides doesn't change the angle - it's the same
>    opening, so the angles are equal.
