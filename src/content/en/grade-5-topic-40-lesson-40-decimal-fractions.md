## Why It Matters

A price tag says $12.50$. A thermometer says $36.6$. A runner's time is
$9.58$ seconds. None of these is written as a fraction with a bar, yet
every one of them is a fraction - a part of a whole. Decimals are
fractions dressed up to look like ordinary numbers, and that costume is
exactly why they're everywhere: they add, compare and round just like
the digits you already know.

## The Big Idea

Go back to the jars: ones, tens, hundreds, each ten times bigger going
left. Now keep going *right* instead. The jar after the ones is ten times
smaller - a tenths-jar. Then a hundredths-jar, then a thousandths-jar.
A decimal point is just the marker that says "the ones-jar is here":
everything to its left is wholes, everything to its right is parts, and
every jar is still ten times the next one along.

So $3.7$ is $3$ wholes and $7$ tenths - the mixed number $3\frac{7}{10}$
with the bar folded away. $0.25$ is no wholes, $2$ tenths and $5$
hundredths - twenty-five hundredths, $\frac{25}{100}$.

## The Rule

A **decimal fraction** is a way to write a fraction, or a mixed number,
whose denominator is $10$, $100$, $1000$, ... - without the bar. The
whole part comes first, then a **decimal point**, then the fractional
part, which has as many digits as the denominator has zeros:

$$
3\frac{7}{10} = 3.7, \qquad \frac{25}{100} = 0.25, \qquad
5\frac{9}{1000} = 5.009
$$

The places after the point are **tenths**, **hundredths**,
**thousandths**, and so on, each ten times smaller than the last.

Reading: $3.7$ is "three point seven" or "three and seven tenths";
$5.009$ is "five and nine thousandths."

Zeros at the end of the fractional part change nothing: $3.7 = 3.70 =
3.700$ - seven tenths is seventy hundredths. But a zero *between* the
point and a digit matters a great deal: $5.009$ is not $5.9$.

## Worked Example

Write $2\frac{41}{1000}$ as a decimal, and write $0.06$ as a common
fraction.

- **$2\frac{41}{1000}$.** The denominator has three zeros, so the
  fractional part needs three digits. $41$ has only two - pad it on the
  left: $041$. Whole part $2$, point, then $041$: $2.041$.
- **Check by reading:** two and forty-one thousandths. Yes.
- **$0.06$.** Two digits after the point mean hundredths. The digits read
  $06$, which is $6$. So $0.06 = \frac{6}{100}$.

And the price tag: $12.50$ is twelve and fifty hundredths - $12\frac{50}{100}$,
or just as well $12\frac{5}{10}$, since $12.50 = 12.5$.

## Common Mistake

Dropping the padding zeros. $2\frac{41}{1000}$ written as $2.41$ says
forty-one *hundredths*, ten times too much. The number of digits after the
point has to match the zeros in the denominator exactly, so a short
numerator gets zeros in front of it. When reading a decimal, count the
places after the point: that count names the denominator.

## The Takeaway

A decimal is a fraction with denominator $10$, $100$, $1000$, ... written
without a bar: wholes before the point, tenths, hundredths, thousandths
after it. The number of digits after the point is the number of zeros in
the denominator - pad with zeros to make it match.

## Check Yourself

1. Write $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{47}{100}$ and $8\frac{5}{100}$ as
   decimals.
2. Write $0.9$, $4.03$ and $0.125$ as common fractions or mixed numbers.
3. Read $17.008$ out loud. What does the $8$ stand for?
4. Are $6.4$ and $6.40$ the same number? Are $6.4$ and $6.04$?
5. A runner's time is $9\frac{58}{100}$ seconds. Write it as a decimal.
6. Someone writes $3\frac{9}{1000}$ as $3.9$. What's the right decimal?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $0.3$, $0.47$, $8.05$.
> 2. $\frac{9}{10}$, $4\frac{3}{100}$, $\frac{125}{1000}$.
> 3. "Seventeen and eight thousandths"; the $8$ is thousandths.
> 4. Yes; no - four tenths against four hundredths.
> 5. $9.58$.
> 6. $3.009$ - the fractional part needs three digits for thousandths.
