## Why It Matters

A wheel touches the road at exactly one point, and at that point the
road is at right angles to the spoke. A belt runs off a pulley along a
tangent. A ball leaves a spinning wheel along a tangent. The line that
just grazes a circle has a name and two clean theorems, and they're
what the next topic needs to fit a circle inside a triangle.

## The Big Idea

Move a secant away from the centre until its two crossing points
merge into one: that's a tangent - a line touching the circle at a
single point. Since it meets the circle once, its distance from the
centre is exactly the radius; and the segment from the centre to the
touching point *is* that distance, so it's perpendicular to the line.
The radius to the point of contact stands at right angles to the
tangent.

From a point outside, two tangents can be drawn, and the two tangent
segments are equal: the two right triangles they make with the centre
share the hypotenuse and have equal legs (radii), so they're
congruent - and the line to the centre bisects the angle between the
tangents.

## The Rule

A **tangent** to a circle is a line that has exactly one common point
with it - the **point of tangency** (point of contact).

**Theorem.** A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the
point of tangency.

**Converse.** A line through a point of the circle perpendicular to the
radius at that point is a tangent.

**Theorem (tangent segments).** If two tangents are drawn to a circle
from an external point $A$, touching at $B$ and $C$, then $AB = AC$, and
the ray $AO$ bisects $\angle BAC$.

*Proof.* $\triangle ABO$ and $\triangle ACO$ are right triangles ($\angle B =
\angle C = 90^\circ$) with common hypotenuse $AO$ and equal legs $OB = OC =
r$. By hypotenuse-and-leg they're congruent, so $AB = AC$ and $\angle
BAO = \angle CAO$. $\square$

## Worked Example

A tangent touches a circle of radius $6$ at $B$, and a point $A$ on the
tangent is $10$ from the centre $O$. Find the tangent segment $AB$ (a
$6, 8, 10$ triangle). Then: from $A$ the two tangents make an angle of
$60^\circ$; find $\angle AOB$.

- **$\angle ABO = 90^\circ$** - radius to the point of tangency.
- **Right triangle $ABO$:** hypotenuse $AO = 10$, leg $OB = 6$; the
  other leg is $8$. So $AB = 8$.
- **Two tangents from $A$:** $AO$ bisects $\angle BAC = 60^\circ$, so $\angle
  BAO = 30^\circ$; in the right triangle, $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$.

Construction: to draw the tangent at a point $B$ of a circle, draw the
radius $OB$ and erect the perpendicular to it at $B$.

## Common Mistake

Assuming a line through a point of the circle is a tangent. Through a
point of the circle pass infinitely many lines; all but one cut the
circle again. Only the one *perpendicular to the radius* at that point
touches it. And "tangent segments equal" applies to the two segments
from the *same* external point - segments from different points have
no reason to match.

## The Takeaway

A tangent meets the circle once and is perpendicular to the radius at
the contact point - and conversely. The two tangent segments from an
outside point are equal, and the line from that point to the centre
bisects the angle between them.

## Check Yourself

1. A tangent touches a circle at $B$. What's the angle between the
   tangent and the radius $OB$?
2. From $A$, tangent segments to a circle are $AB$ and $AC$, $AB = 7$.
   Find $AC$.
3. $A$ is $13$ from the centre of a circle of radius $5$; the tangent
   from $A$ touches at $B$. Find $AB$ (a $5, 12, 13$ triangle).
4. Two tangents from $A$ make $\angle BAC = 40^\circ$. Find $\angle BAO$ and
   $\angle AOB$.
5. How would you construct the tangent at a given point of a circle?
6. Someone says any line through a point of the circle is a tangent
   there. Why not?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $90^\circ$.
> 2. $7$.
> 3. $12$.
> 4. $20^\circ$; $70^\circ$.
> 5. Draw the radius to the point and the perpendicular to it there.
> 6. Only the line perpendicular to the radius touches once; the others
>    cut the circle again.
