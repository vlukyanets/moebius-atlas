## Why It Matters

You bought a notebook for $48$ hryvnias and a pen for $27$ - how much do you
pay at the till? Your team scored $3$ goals in the first half and $2$ in the
second - what's the final score? Every time two amounts get put together,
that's addition. It's the first thing anyone learns to do with numbers, and
every other operation - subtraction, multiplication, even powers - is built
on top of it.

## The Big Idea

Go back to the jars from the natural numbers topic: a ones-jar, a tens-jar,
a hundreds-jar, and so on. Adding two numbers means pouring the second
number's marbles into the first number's jars, jar by jar - ones into the
ones-jar, tens into the tens-jar. Sometimes a jar overflows: it ends up with
ten or more marbles. Then the old rule kicks in - ten marbles get bundled
up and moved one jar to the left. That bundle is what everyone calls
"carrying the one." It's not a trick to memorise; it's just the jars doing
what jars do.

## The Rule

The numbers being added are called **terms**, and the result is the
**sum**:

$$
48 + 27 = 75
$$

Here $48$ and $27$ are the terms, and $75$ is the sum.

To add numbers in a column, write them one under the other with ones under
ones, tens under tens, hundreds under hundreds. Add each column from the
right. If a column adds up to $10$ or more, write down its last digit and
carry $1$ into the next column on the left.

Addition has two properties that let you rearrange a sum any way you like:

- **Commutative property** - swapping the terms doesn't change the sum:
  $a + b = b + a$. Here $a$ and $b$ stand for any two numbers.
- **Associative property** - the terms can be grouped in any way:
  $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$. The brackets say which addition to do first.

Together they mean you can add a list of numbers in whatever order is
easiest. And adding zero changes nothing: $a + 0 = a$.

## Worked Example

Find $4786 + 2359$.

- **Line the numbers up** so that ones sit under ones and tens under tens.
- **Ones column:** $6 + 9 = 15$. That's ten or more, so write $5$ and carry
  $1$ into the tens column - the ten marbles have been bundled up and moved
  one jar left.
- **Tens column:** $8 + 5 = 13$, plus the carried $1$ makes $14$. Write $4$,
  carry $1$.
- **Hundreds column:** $7 + 3 = 10$, plus the carried $1$ makes $11$. Write
  $1$, carry $1$.
- **Thousands column:** $4 + 2 = 6$, plus the carried $1$ makes $7$. Write
  $7$ - nothing left to carry.

So $4786 + 2359 = 7145$.

Now a sum that the properties make easy: $37 + 189 + 63$. Adding left to
right is clumsy. But swap the terms and group them: $(37 + 63) + 189 =
100 + 189 = 289$. Same terms, same sum - just a friendlier order.

## Common Mistake

Losing the carry. In $4786 + 2359$, the ones column gives $15$: some people
write down the $5$ and forget the $1$ that has to go into the tens. Then
the tens column gives $13$ instead of $14$, and every digit from there on
is wrong. Write the carried $1$ above the next column the moment it appears -
don't keep it in your head.

## The Takeaway

Adding in a column is just adding the digits of each place, and carrying
whenever a place fills up to ten. And because the order of the terms and
the way they're grouped never change the sum, you're free to add in whatever
order is easiest.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $3648 + 1975$.
2. Find $58 + 276 + 42$ in the most convenient order.
3. Name the terms and the sum in $125 + 75 = 200$.
4. Which property says that $15 + 48 = 48 + 15$?
5. A movie was watched $2\,850\,000$ times on Monday and $1\,470\,000$ times
   on Tuesday. How many times was it watched over the two days?
6. Someone added $4786 + 2359$ and got $7135$. Which column did they make
   the mistake in?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $5623$.
> 2. $(58 + 42) + 276 = 100 + 276 = 376$.
> 3. Terms $125$ and $75$, sum $200$.
> 4. The commutative property - the terms are swapped and the sum stays the
>    same.
> 5. $2\,850\,000 + 1\,470\,000 = 4\,320\,000$ times.
> 6. The tens column: the correct answer is $7145$, so the tens digit should
>    be $4$ - the carry from the ones column was lost.
