## Why It Matters

How much paint for the wall? How many tiles for the floor? How big is the
flat, the field, the screen? Perimeter told you about the edge; none of
these questions is about the edge - they're about how much surface there
is inside. That's area, and for rectangles it's one multiplication away.

## The Big Idea

To measure a surface you need a unit of surface, and the natural one is a
small square - one centimetre by one centimetre, say. Area is how many
of those squares it takes to cover the figure with no gaps and no
overlaps. Cover a rectangle $5$ cm long and $3$ cm wide with such
squares: they line up in $3$ rows of $5$. That's $5 \cdot 3 = 15$ squares -
and there's the formula. The area of a rectangle is its length times its
width, because that's exactly how many unit squares fit in a grid that
size.

## The Rule

The **area** of a figure is the number of unit squares that cover it. The
units are **square** units: a square centimetre, $\text{cm}^2$, is a
square with side $1$ cm; likewise $\text{mm}^2$, $\text{dm}^2$,
$\text{m}^2$, $\text{km}^2$. The little $2$ is the same as in a power -
a square has side times side.

Area of a rectangle with length $a$ and width $b$:

$$
S = a \cdot b
$$

Area of a square with side $a$:

$$
S = a \cdot a = a^2
$$

That's why $a^2$ is called "$a$ squared."

Converting units: a square with side $1$ dm is $10$ cm by $10$ cm, so
$1\ \text{dm}^2 = 100\ \text{cm}^2$. In the same way
$1\ \text{cm}^2 = 100\ \text{mm}^2$, $1\ \text{m}^2 = 100\ \text{dm}^2 =
10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$, $1\ \text{km}^2 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$. Land
is also measured in **ares** ($1$ a $= 100\ \text{m}^2$) and **hectares**
($1$ ha $= 100$ a $= 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$).

Congruent figures have equal areas, and a figure cut into pieces has area
equal to the sum of the pieces.

## Worked Example

A room is $6$ m long and $4$ m wide. The floor is tiled with square tiles
of side $50$ cm. How many tiles are needed?

- **Area of the floor:** $S = 6 \cdot 4 = 24\ \text{m}^2$.
- **Area of one tile:** side $50$ cm gives $50 \cdot 50 = 2500\
  \text{cm}^2$.
- **Same units.** $24\ \text{m}^2 = 24 \cdot 10\,000 = 240\,000\
  \text{cm}^2$.
- **Divide:** $240\,000 : 2500 = 96$ tiles.

Now backwards. A rectangle has area $72\ \text{cm}^2$ and width $6$ cm.
The length is $72 : 6 = 12$ cm.

## Common Mistake

Mixing up area and perimeter. A square with side $4$ cm has perimeter
$16$ cm and area $16\ \text{cm}^2$ - the same number, and that's a
coincidence that misleads people into thinking they're the same thing.
Try side $5$: perimeter $20$ cm, area $25\ \text{cm}^2$. Perimeter is the
length of the edge, measured in cm; area is the surface inside, measured
in $\text{cm}^2$. Different questions, different units.

## The Takeaway

Area counts the unit squares that cover a figure. For a rectangle that's
length times width; for a square it's the side squared - and that's where
the name "squared" comes from.

## Check Yourself

1. Find the area of a rectangle $9$ cm by $7$ cm.
2. Find the area of a square with side $11$ dm.
3. A rectangle has area $96\ \text{cm}^2$ and length $12$ cm. Find its
   width and its perimeter.
4. Express $3\ \text{m}^2$ in $\text{cm}^2$, and $5$ ha in $\text{m}^2$.
5. A field is $300$ m by $200$ m. What's its area in hectares?
6. Someone says a $4 \times 4$ square "has perimeter and area both
   $16$, so they're equal." What's wrong with that?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $63\ \text{cm}^2$.
> 2. $121\ \text{dm}^2$.
> 3. Width $96 : 12 = 8$ cm; perimeter $2 \cdot (12 + 8) = 40$ cm.
> 4. $30\,000\ \text{cm}^2$; $50\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
> 5. $60\,000\ \text{m}^2 = 6$ ha.
> 6. They're different quantities in different units - $16$ cm of edge and
>    $16\ \text{cm}^2$ of surface; a $5 \times 5$ square shows the numbers
>    differ in general.
