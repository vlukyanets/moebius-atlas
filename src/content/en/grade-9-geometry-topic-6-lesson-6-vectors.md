## Why It Matters

"Walk $3$ km" is not enough - which way? Velocity, force,
displacement, wind: each has a size *and* a direction, and a plain
number can't hold both. A vector can. Vectors are the language of
physics from grade 9 on, and in geometry they give a second way to
prove things - by adding arrows rather than chasing angles. This
topic is the vocabulary: what a vector is, when two are equal, when
they point the same way.

## The Big Idea

A vector is an arrow: a segment with a chosen start and end. What
matters about it is only how long it is and which way it points -
*not* where it sits. Slide an arrow across the page without turning
it and it's the same vector, because the same displacement from
anywhere takes you the same distance in the same direction. That's
why two vectors are equal when they have the same length and the
same direction, however far apart they're drawn. Vectors lying on
parallel lines are collinear: they point the same way or opposite
ways, and nothing in between.

## The Rule

A **vector** is a directed segment. $\overrightarrow{AB}$ starts at $A$
and ends at $B$; it is also written $\vec{a}$. Its **magnitude**
(modulus, length) is $|\overrightarrow{AB}| = AB$.

The **zero vector** $\vec{0}$ has coinciding start and end, length $0$
and no direction.

Nonzero vectors are **collinear** if they lie on one line or on
parallel lines. Collinear vectors are **co-directed** ($\vec{a}
\upuparrows \vec{b}$) if they point the same way, **oppositely directed**
($\vec{a} \uparrow\downarrow \vec{b}$) otherwise.

Two vectors are **equal** if they are co-directed and have equal
magnitudes. Equal vectors are obtained from each other by a
translation; from any point exactly one vector equal to a given one
can be drawn.

## Worked Example

$ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Which of $\overrightarrow{AB}$,
$\overrightarrow{DC}$, $\overrightarrow{CD}$, $\overrightarrow{AD}$ are
collinear, co-directed, equal? Then: a vector has magnitude $5$ and
starts at $A(1; 2)$ pointing straight up; where does it end? Then: are
$\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BA}$ equal?

- **Collinear:** $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\overrightarrow{DC}$ and
  $\overrightarrow{CD}$ all lie on the parallel sides $AB \parallel DC$.
  $\overrightarrow{AD}$ is not collinear with them.
- **Co-directed:** $\overrightarrow{AB} \upuparrows \overrightarrow{DC}$ (both
  run "from the $A$-$D$ side toward the $B$-$C$ side");
  $\overrightarrow{AB} \uparrow\downarrow \overrightarrow{CD}$.
- **Equal:** $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{DC}$ - co-directed and
  $AB = DC$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram). $\overrightarrow{AB} \neq
  \overrightarrow{CD}$: same length, opposite direction.
- **Straight up by $5$:** ends at $(1; 7)$.
- **$\overrightarrow{AB}$ vs $\overrightarrow{BA}$:** same length, opposite
  direction - not equal. $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB}$.

The parallelogram is the picture to keep: opposite sides, read the
same way round, are equal vectors.

## Common Mistake

Calling two vectors equal because they have the same length. $|
\overrightarrow{AB}| = |\overrightarrow{BA}|$, yet they point opposite
ways and are not equal - equality needs direction *and* magnitude.
Conversely, thinking two arrows in different places must be
different: $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{DC}$ in the
parallelogram are the same vector, drawn twice. A vector is a
displacement, not a location.

## The Takeaway

A vector is an arrow known by its length and direction only; equal
vectors are co-directed with equal lengths, wherever they sit.
Collinear vectors lie on parallel lines and are co- or oppositely
directed. $\overrightarrow{BA}$ is the opposite of $\overrightarrow{AB}$.

## Check Yourself

1. In parallelogram $ABCD$, name a vector equal to $\overrightarrow{BC}$.
2. In the same parallelogram, is $\overrightarrow{AB}$ collinear with
   $\overrightarrow{BC}$?
3. Is the zero vector collinear with every vector? What is its
   magnitude?
4. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Compare $\overrightarrow{AM}$ and
   $\overrightarrow{MB}$.
5. Can two collinear vectors of equal length be unequal?
6. Someone says $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{CD}$ whenever $AB =
   CD$. Give a counterexample.

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $\overrightarrow{AD}$.
> 2. No - $AB$ and $BC$ are not parallel.
> 3. By convention, yes; $0$.
> 4. Equal - same direction, $AM = MB$.
> 5. Yes, if oppositely directed.
> 6. A square $ABCD$: $AB = CD$, but $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and
>    $\overrightarrow{CD}$ point opposite ways.
