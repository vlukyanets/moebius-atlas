## Why It Matters

A fitness tracker draws your heart rate through the day. A weather app
draws tomorrow's temperature hour by hour. A car's dashboard draws fuel
against distance. None of them hands you a table of numbers - they hand
you a line, because a line tells the story at a glance: when it rose,
when it fell, when it stood still. A graph is a dependency between two
quantities drawn on the coordinate plane.

## The Big Idea

Take a rule that ties two quantities together - "a car at $60$ km/h
covers $60t$ km in $t$ hours." Pick values of $t$, compute the distances,
and write each pair as a point: $(1; 60)$, $(2; 120)$, $(3; 180)$. Plot them
on a coordinate plane, time along the $x$-axis and distance up the
$y$-axis. They line up - a straight line through the origin. That line
is the graph of the dependency, and every point on it is a "when this,
then that."

Direct proportionality always draws as a straight line through the
origin. Inverse proportionality draws as a curve that swoops down and
never touches the axes. Other dependencies draw other shapes, and the
shape *is* the information.

## The Rule

A **graph of a dependency** between two quantities is the set of points
on the coordinate plane whose abscissa is a value of the first quantity
and whose ordinate is the corresponding value of the second.

To draw one from a rule: make a table of values, plot the points, join
them (smoothly, or with segments if the quantity changes in jumps).

To read one: for a given $x$, go up to the graph and across to the
$y$-axis; for a given $y$, go across to the graph and down to the
$x$-axis. Rising to the right means the second quantity grows as the
first does; falling means it shrinks; horizontal means it stays.

Graphs of $y = kx$ are straight lines through the origin; graphs of
$y = \frac{k}{x}$ are curves in two quadrants that approach the axes
without touching them.

## Worked Example

A cyclist rides at $15$ km/h. Draw the graph of distance against time
for the first $4$ hours, and use it to answer: how far after $2.5$ hours,
and when has she covered $45$ km?

- **Rule:** $s = 15t$. **Table:** $t = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ gives $s = 0, 15, 30,
  45, 60$.
- **Plot:** $(0; 0)$, $(1; 15)$, $(2; 30)$, $(3; 45)$, $(4; 60)$ - a
  straight line through the origin.
- **Read $t = 2.5$:** go up from $2.5$ to the line, across: $37.5$ km.
- **Read $s = 45$:** across from $45$ to the line, down: $3$ hours.

Now a graph of temperature through a day, read off: it rises from
$-3^\circ$ at $6$ am to $8^\circ$ at $2$ pm, then falls to $1^\circ$ by
midnight. The highest point of the line is the warmest moment; where the
line crosses the $x$-axis, the temperature was exactly $0^\circ$.

## Common Mistake

Reading the graph against the wrong axis, or joining points that
shouldn't be joined. Distance is on the vertical axis, so "how far at
$2.5$ hours" is read *up* then *across* - not across then up. And a
graph of "number of tickets sold each day" is separate dots, not a
line: there's no half-day between Monday and Tuesday. Join points only
when the quantity changes continuously.

## The Takeaway

A graph turns a rule between two quantities into a picture: each pair
of values is a point, and the points trace a shape. Straight through
the origin means direct proportionality; a swooping curve means
inverse. Read it by going up to the line and across, and let the rises
and falls tell the story.

## Check Yourself

1. A shop sells apples at $30$ hryvnias a kilogram. Make a table for
   $1$ to $4$ kg and describe the graph.
2. What does the graph of a direct proportionality look like?
3. On a distance-time graph, what does a horizontal stretch mean?
4. From the cyclist's graph ($15$ km/h), how far has she gone after
   $3.5$ hours?
5. A graph of a pool's water level rises steadily for $2$ hours, stays
   flat for $1$ hour, then falls steadily. Tell the story in words.
6. Someone draws the number of pupils absent each day of the week as a
   smooth curve. What's wrong with that?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $(1; 30)$, $(2; 60)$, $(3; 90)$, $(4; 120)$ - a straight line through
>    the origin.
> 2. A straight line through the origin.
> 3. The distance isn't changing - the mover has stopped.
> 4. $52.5$ km.
> 5. Water was poured in for $2$ hours, then nothing happened for an
>    hour, then it was drained.
> 6. Absences are whole numbers on separate days; there's no value
>    "between" days, so the graph should be separate points.
