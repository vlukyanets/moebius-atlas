## Why It Matters

A taxi charges $40$ hryvnias to start plus $12$ per kilometre. How much is
a $5$-kilometre ride? A $9$-kilometre one? A $20$-kilometre one? You could
work each out from scratch - or write the rule once, $40 + 12 \cdot k$,
and just drop in the number of kilometres. A rule with a letter in it
answers every version of the question at once. That's what formulas are
for, and it's the first step into algebra.

## The Big Idea

An expression is a calculation written down but not yet done. $40 + 12
\cdot 5$ is a numeric expression: every part of it is a number, so it has
one definite value, $100$. Replace the $5$ with a letter - $40 + 12 \cdot
k$ - and it becomes an algebraic expression: a calculation waiting for a
number. Put $k = 9$ in and it's worth $148$; put $k = 20$ in and it's
$280$. The letter is a blank, and the expression is the same recipe every
time.

A formula is an algebraic expression that's been given a name: $C = 40 +
12 \cdot k$, where $C$ is the cost. Now the rule has two sides, and
either can be found from the other.

## The Rule

A **numeric expression** is a record made of numbers, operation signs and
brackets, like $(120 - 45) : 5 + 2^3$. Its **value** is what you get when
you carry out the operations. Remember the order: brackets first, then
powers, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and
subtraction left to right.

An **algebraic expression** also contains letters, called **variables**:
$40 + 12 \cdot k$, $a \cdot b$, $2 \cdot (x + y)$. It has no single value -
it has a value for each choice of the letters. To find it, substitute the
numbers and calculate.

A **formula** is an equality that says how one quantity is found from
others. A few you'll use all the time:

- Distance: $s = v \cdot t$ - speed times time.
- Perimeter of a rectangle: $P = 2 \cdot (a + b)$.
- Cost: $C = p \cdot n$ - price times quantity.

Multiplication signs next to letters are usually dropped: $12k$ means
$12 \cdot k$, and $ab$ means $a \cdot b$.

## Worked Example

Find the value of $3a + b^2 - 10$ when $a = 7$ and $b = 4$.

- **Substitute:** every $a$ becomes $7$, every $b$ becomes $4$:
  $3 \cdot 7 + 4^2 - 10$.
- **Power first:** $4^2 = 16$. Now it's $3 \cdot 7 + 16 - 10$.
- **Multiplication:** $3 \cdot 7 = 21$. Now $21 + 16 - 10$.
- **Left to right:** $21 + 16 = 37$, $37 - 10 = 27$.

So the value is $27$.

Now the formula $s = v \cdot t$. A cyclist rides at $v = 15$ km/h for
$t = 3$ h: $s = 15 \cdot 3 = 45$ km. The same formula answers the reverse
question: at $15$ km/h, how long does $90$ km take? $90 = 15 \cdot t$, so
$t = 90 : 15 = 6$ hours.

## Common Mistake

Reading $3a$ as "$3$ and $a$ side by side." When $a = 7$, some people turn
$3a$ into $37$. But $3a$ is a product, $3 \cdot a$, so it's $21$. The
missing dot is only a shortcut in writing - the multiplication is still
there.

## The Takeaway

An expression is a calculation written down; a letter in it stands for any
number you choose to put there. A formula is such an expression with a
name attached - one rule that answers a whole family of questions.

## Check Yourself

1. Find the value of $(85 - 37) : 4 + 5^2$.
2. Find the value of $2x + 3y$ when $x = 8$, $y = 5$.
3. Write an expression for "the sum of $a$ and $b$, multiplied by $6$."
4. A taxi charges $40 + 12k$ hryvnias for $k$ kilometres. How much is
   $15$ km?
5. Using $P = 2(a + b)$, find the perimeter of a rectangle with sides
   $9$ cm and $4$ cm.
6. A car drives at $v = 80$ km/h and covers $s = 320$ km. Use $s = vt$ to
   find the time.

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $48 : 4 + 25 = 12 + 25 = 37$.
> 2. $2 \cdot 8 + 3 \cdot 5 = 16 + 15 = 31$.
> 3. $6(a + b)$, or $(a + b) \cdot 6$.
> 4. $40 + 12 \cdot 15 = 40 + 180 = 220$ hryvnias.
> 5. $P = 2 \cdot (9 + 4) = 2 \cdot 13 = 26$ cm.
> 6. $320 = 80 \cdot t$, so $t = 320 : 80 = 4$ hours.
