## Why It Matters

How many $\frac{3}{4}$-litre bottles can you fill from $6$ litres? A
$\frac{5}{6}$-metre strip is cut into pieces of $\frac{1}{12}$ m - how
many pieces? Half a cake is shared among three - how much each? Each of
these divides by, or into, a fraction. There's a rule that makes all of
them a multiplication - and reciprocals are why it works.

## The Big Idea

Dividing by $2$ is the same as multiplying by $\frac{1}{2}$: halving. So
dividing by a number ought to be multiplying by its reciprocal - and it
is. Dividing $6$ by $\frac{3}{4}$ asks "how many three-quarters fit into
six?" There are $4$ quarters in each litre, so $24$ quarters in six
litres, and grouping them in threes gives $8$. That's $6 \cdot \frac{4}{3}
= 8$: multiply by the flipped fraction.

The reason: dividing by $\frac{3}{4}$ should undo multiplying by
$\frac{3}{4}$. Multiplying by $\frac{4}{3}$ does exactly that, because
$\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = 1$.

## The Rule

To divide by a fraction, **multiply by its reciprocal**:

$$
\frac{a}{b} : \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c}
$$

The first fraction stays as it is; only the divisor is flipped. Mixed
numbers become improper fractions first, and a natural number is a
fraction over $1$. Cancel before multiplying.

Dividing by a fraction less than $1$ makes a number *bigger*; dividing
by a fraction greater than $1$ makes it smaller. Division by zero is
still impossible.

## Worked Example

Find $\frac{5}{6} : \frac{10}{9}$.

- **Flip the divisor:** $\frac{10}{9}$ becomes $\frac{9}{10}$.
- **Multiply:** $\frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{9}{10}$.
- **Cancel:** $5$ with $10$, $9$ with $6$: $\frac{1 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 2}$.
- **Result:** $\frac{3}{4}$.

Check by multiplying back: $\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{10}{9} = \frac{30}{36}
= \frac{5}{6}$.

The strip: $\frac{5}{6} : \frac{1}{12} = \frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{12}{1} =
10$ pieces. The cake: $\frac{1}{2} : 3 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} =
\frac{1}{6}$ each. Mixed numbers: $2\frac{1}{4} : 1\frac{1}{2} =
\frac{9}{4} : \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{2} =
1\frac{1}{2}$.

## Common Mistake

Flipping the wrong fraction - or both. $\frac{5}{6} : \frac{10}{9}$ is
not $\frac{6}{5} \cdot \frac{10}{9}$, and not $\frac{6}{5} \cdot
\frac{9}{10}$. Only the number you divide *by* gets turned over; the
dividend is left exactly as it was. Say it as a sentence - "keep, flip,
multiply" - and the keep comes first.

## The Takeaway

Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal: keep the first
fraction, flip the second, multiply, cancel. Dividing by something less
than $1$ gives more than you started with.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $\frac{2}{3} : \frac{4}{9}$.
2. Find $\frac{7}{8} : 14$.
3. Find $5 : \frac{5}{6}$.
4. Find $3\frac{1}{3} : 2\frac{2}{9}$.
5. How many $\frac{3}{4}$-litre bottles can be filled from $6$ litres?
6. Someone computes $\frac{5}{6} : \frac{10}{9}$ as $\frac{6}{5} \cdot
   \frac{9}{10} = \frac{27}{25}$. What went wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{4} = \frac{3}{2}$.
> 2. $\frac{7}{8} \cdot \frac{1}{14} = \frac{1}{16}$.
> 3. $5 \cdot \frac{6}{5} = 6$.
> 4. $\frac{10}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{20} = \frac{3}{2} = 1\frac{1}{2}$.
> 5. $6 : \frac{3}{4} = 8$.
> 6. The dividend was flipped too; only the divisor flips: $\frac{5}{6}
>    \cdot \frac{9}{10} = \frac{3}{4}$.
