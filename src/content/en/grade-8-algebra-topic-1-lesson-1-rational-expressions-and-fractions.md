## Why It Matters

Speed is distance over time: $\frac{s}{t}$. Average price is total cost
over quantity. Resistance in parallel is $\frac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$.
The moment a variable lands in a denominator, you have a rational
fraction - and last year's algebra, which was all polynomials, stops
being enough. This year is the algebra of fractions with letters in
them, and it begins where grade 7 ended: knowing when such a fraction
means anything at all.

## The Big Idea

A polynomial can be evaluated for any value of its variables. A
fraction with a polynomial underneath can't: wherever the denominator
is zero, the fraction is undefined. So every rational fraction comes
with a domain - all the values except those that kill the
denominator - and finding it means solving "denominator $= 0$", which
usually means factoring.

Everything you did with ordinary fractions carries over: cancel,
bring to a common denominator, add, multiply, divide. But every step
happens over the domain, and the first thing to do with any fraction
is to write down where it lives.

## The Rule

A **rational expression** is built from numbers and variables by
addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. An **integer**
rational expression has no division by a variable expression; a
**fractional** one does.

A **rational fraction** is a fraction $\frac{P}{Q}$ whose numerator $P$
and denominator $Q$ are polynomials. Examples: $\frac{x + 1}{x - 2}$,
$\frac{a^2 - b^2}{ab}$, $\frac{5}{y}$.

The **domain** (admissible values) of a rational fraction is the set of
values of the variables for which the denominator is not zero. The
domain of an integer expression is all numbers.

A rational fraction equals $0$ exactly when its numerator is $0$ **and**
its denominator is not.

## Worked Example

Find the domain of $\frac{x + 1}{x - 2}$, $\frac{3}{x^2 - 9}$ and
$\frac{a}{a^2 + 1}$; then find where $\frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2}$ equals zero.

- **$\frac{x + 1}{x - 2}$:** $x - 2 = 0$ at $x = 2$. Domain: all $x \neq 2$.
- **$\frac{3}{x^2 - 9}$:** $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3) = 0$ at $x = 3$ or
  $x = -3$. Domain: all $x$ except $\pm 3$.
- **$\frac{a}{a^2 + 1}$:** $a^2 + 1 \geq 1$, never zero. Domain: all
  numbers - a fraction that's safe everywhere.
- **$\frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2} = 0$:** numerator $(x - 2)(x + 2) = 0$ at $x =
  \pm 2$; but $x = -2$ is outside the domain. Only $x = 2$.

## Common Mistake

Setting the numerator to zero and forgetting the denominator. $x =
-2$ makes the numerator of $\frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2}$ vanish - and the
denominator too, so the fraction isn't zero there, it's undefined. A
zero of a fraction must lie in its domain. Write the domain first;
then any candidate outside it is discarded automatically.

## The Takeaway

A rational fraction is polynomial over polynomial, and it lives only
where the denominator isn't zero - find that set by factoring the
denominator. It's zero where the numerator is zero *within* the
domain.

## Check Yourself

1. Which are rational fractions: $\frac{x}{3}$, $\frac{3}{x}$, $x^2 -
   \frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$?
2. Find the domain of $\frac{2x}{x + 5}$.
3. Find the domain of $\frac{1}{x^2 - 5x}$.
4. Find the domain of $\frac{x - 1}{x^2 + 4}$.
5. For which $x$ is $\frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$ equal to zero?
6. Someone says $\frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = 0$ at $x = 1$ and $x = -1$. Which
   answer is wrong, and why?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $\frac{3}{x}$ and $\frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ (the others are integer
>    expressions).
> 2. $x \neq -5$.
> 3. $x(x - 5) \neq 0$: $x \neq 0$ and $x \neq 5$.
> 4. All numbers.
> 5. $x = -1$ only.
> 6. $x = 1$ - the denominator is zero there, so the fraction is
>    undefined, not zero.
