## Why It Matters

Apples are $24.5$ hryvnias a kilo and you're buying $1.6$ kg - how much?
A room is $4.2$ m by $3.5$ m - what's its area? A car does $12.5$ km on a
litre and has $30.4$ litres - how far can it go? Price times quantity,
length times width, rate times amount: the moment the numbers have
decimal points, this is the multiplication you need.

## The Big Idea

Multiply as if the points weren't there, then put the point back. That's
the whole method, and here's why it works. $24.5$ is $245$ tenths, and
$1.6$ is $16$ tenths. Tenths times tenths are hundredths - a tenth of a
tenth - so $245 \cdot 16 = 3920$ *hundredths*, which is $39.20$. Two
decimal places in the factors together, two decimal places in the
answer. The digits come from the natural-number product; the place of
the point comes from counting.

## The Rule

To multiply two decimals:

1. **Multiply them as natural numbers**, ignoring the points.
2. **Count the decimal places** in both factors and add the counts.
3. **Put the point in the product** so that it has that many decimal
   places, counting from the right. If there aren't enough digits, add
   zeros on the left.

$$
24.5 \cdot 1.6: \quad 245 \cdot 16 = 3920, \quad 1 + 1 = 2 \text{ places}
\quad \Rightarrow \quad 39.20 = 39.2
$$

Multiplying by $10$, $100$, $1000$ moves the point to the *right* by one,
two, three places: $3.75 \cdot 100 = 375$. Multiplying by $0.1$, $0.01$,
$0.001$ moves it to the *left*: $375 \cdot 0.01 = 3.75$.

The commutative, associative and distributive properties work exactly as
before.

## Worked Example

Find $0.42 \cdot 2.5$.

- **Ignore the points:** $42 \cdot 25$. $42 \cdot 25 = 1050$.
- **Count places:** $0.42$ has two, $2.5$ has one - three in total.
- **Place the point** three from the right in $1050$: $1.050$, which is
  $1.05$.

So $0.42 \cdot 2.5 = 1.05$. Sanity check: $0.42$ is a bit less than a
half, and half of $2.5$ is $1.25$ - so a bit less than $1.25$ is right.

Now one with a leading zero: $0.03 \cdot 0.2$. $3 \cdot 2 = 6$; places
$2 + 1 = 3$; but $6$ has only one digit, so pad: $0.006$.

## Common Mistake

Lining up the points as in addition. Multiplication doesn't line
anything up - the points are ignored while multiplying and placed by
count afterwards. Someone who "aligns" $24.5$ and $1.6$ and then writes
the point under the points gets $392.0$, ten times too big. The count of
decimal places, not the position of the columns, is what places the
point.

## The Takeaway

Multiply the digits as natural numbers, then give the product as many
decimal places as the two factors had between them. Multiplying by ten,
a hundred, a thousand just slides the point to the right.

2. Find $1.25 \cdot 0.8$.

4. Find $8.235 \cdot 100$ and $8.235 \cdot 0.01$.
