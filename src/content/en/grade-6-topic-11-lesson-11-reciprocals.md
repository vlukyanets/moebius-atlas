## Why It Matters

Multiply $\frac{3}{4}$ by $\frac{4}{3}$ and you get exactly $1$. That's
not a coincidence you can ignore - it's the key that unlocks dividing by
a fraction, which is the next topic. Just as subtraction undoes addition
by way of opposite numbers, division undoes multiplication by way of
reciprocals: to divide by a number, multiply by the one that cancels it
to $1$.

## The Big Idea

Flip a fraction upside down and multiply it by the original:
$\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{12}{12} = 1$. The top of one is the
bottom of the other, so everything cancels. Every fraction has such a
partner, and the two are called reciprocals - each is the other's
"undo." Multiplying by $\frac{3}{4}$ shrinks a number to three quarters
of itself; multiplying by $\frac{4}{3}$ stretches it back to where it
was.

Natural numbers have reciprocals too: $5$ is $\frac{5}{1}$, so its
reciprocal is $\frac{1}{5}$. Only zero is left out - nothing times zero
gives $1$.

## The Rule

Two numbers are **reciprocals** (mutually inverse) if their product is
$1$:

$$
\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{b}{a} = 1
$$

The reciprocal of $\frac{a}{b}$ is $\frac{b}{a}$ - the fraction turned
upside down. The reciprocal of a natural number $n$ is $\frac{1}{n}$. For
a mixed number, convert to an improper fraction first, then flip. For a
decimal, write it as a fraction first.

The number $1$ is its own reciprocal. Zero has no reciprocal.

## Worked Example

Find the reciprocals of $\frac{5}{8}$, $7$, $2\frac{1}{3}$ and $0.4$.

- $\frac{5}{8}$: flip it - $\frac{8}{5}$. Check: $\frac{5}{8} \cdot
  \frac{8}{5} = 1$.
- $7 = \frac{7}{1}$: flip - $\frac{1}{7}$.
- $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$: flip - $\frac{3}{7}$.
- $0.4 = \frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}$: flip - $\frac{5}{2} = 2.5$. Check:
  $0.4 \cdot 2.5 = 1$.

Now a small equation: $\frac{3}{4} \cdot x = 1$. The number that
multiplies $\frac{3}{4}$ to give $1$ is its reciprocal: $x = \frac{4}{3}$.

## Common Mistake

Flipping a mixed number as it stands. The reciprocal of $2\frac{1}{3}$
is not $2\frac{3}{1}$ or $\frac{1}{2}\frac{3}{1}$ - those aren't even
numbers written properly. Convert to $\frac{7}{3}$ first, then flip to
$\frac{3}{7}$. And check: $\frac{7}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{7} = 1$ - the product
being $1$ is the definition, and it's always available as a test.

## The Takeaway

Reciprocals multiply to $1$: a fraction and the same fraction upside
down. Natural numbers flip to $\frac{1}{n}$, mixed numbers and decimals
get written as fractions first, and zero has none.

## Check Yourself

1. Find the reciprocal of $\frac{9}{2}$.
2. Find the reciprocal of $12$.
3. Find the reciprocal of $1\frac{3}{5}$.
4. Find the reciprocal of $0.25$.
5. Solve $\frac{5}{6} \cdot x = 1$.
6. Someone says the reciprocal of $3\frac{1}{2}$ is $3\frac{2}{1}$.
   What's the right answer?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $\frac{2}{9}$.
> 2. $\frac{1}{12}$.
> 3. $\frac{5}{8}$ - since $1\frac{3}{5} = \frac{8}{5}$.
> 4. $4$ - since $0.25 = \frac{1}{4}$.
> 5. $x = \frac{6}{5}$.
> 6. $3\frac{1}{2} = \frac{7}{2}$, so the reciprocal is $\frac{2}{7}$.
