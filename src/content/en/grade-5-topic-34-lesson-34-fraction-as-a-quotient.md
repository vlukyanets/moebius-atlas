## Why It Matters

Three friends share two chocolate bars equally. With natural numbers,
$2 : 3$ has no answer - two doesn't go into three parts. But the friends
don't care about that: they cut each bar into three and take two pieces
each. Everyone gets $\frac{2}{3}$ of a bar. The fraction bar *is* a
division sign, and once you see that, every division that "didn't work"
suddenly has an answer.

## The Big Idea

Two bars, three people. Cut each bar into $3$ equal pieces - six pieces,
each a third of a bar. Deal them out: two each. So each person gets two
thirds: $2 : 3 = \frac{2}{3}$. The number being divided lands on top,
the number you divide by lands on the bottom.

This works even when the division *does* come out whole. $12 : 4$ is $3$;
and $\frac{12}{4}$, twelve quarters, is also $3$ - four quarters make one
whole, so twelve make three. Fractions don't replace division; they
finish it.

## The Rule

The quotient of two natural numbers can be written as a fraction whose
numerator is the dividend and whose denominator is the divisor:

$$
a : b = \frac{a}{b}
$$

So a fraction bar means "divided by," and every division has a value -
a natural number if it comes out exact, a fraction if it doesn't.

Two things follow:

- Any natural number is a fraction with denominator $1$: $7 = \frac{7}{1}$.
- A fraction whose numerator is a multiple of its denominator is a
  natural number: $\frac{12}{4} = 3$, $\frac{20}{5} = 4$.

## Worked Example

Five identical pies are shared equally among eight people. How much pie
does each person get?

- **It's a division:** $5$ pies into $8$ equal shares, $5 : 8$.
- **Write it as a fraction:** $5 : 8 = \frac{5}{8}$.
- **Picture it.** Cut each pie into $8$ slices - forty slices - and give
  everyone $5$. Five eighths of a pie each.

Now the other way. Is $\frac{56}{7}$ a natural number? $56 : 7 = 8$
exactly, so yes: $\frac{56}{7} = 8$.

And a mixed case: a $13$-metre rope is cut into $4$ equal parts. Each
part is $13 : 4 = \frac{13}{4}$ m - more than $3$ m, since $\frac{12}{4}$
would be exactly $3$.

## Common Mistake

Putting the numbers the wrong way up. $2 : 3$ is $\frac{2}{3}$ - dividend
on top. Write $\frac{3}{2}$ and you've said each friend gets one and a
half bars from two bars shared three ways, which can't be right: three
people can't each get more than one bar out of two. The size check
settles it - dividing a small number by a big one gives less than $1$.

## The Takeaway

A fraction is a division that's been written down instead of carried
out: numerator over denominator is dividend over divisor. Every natural
number is a fraction over $1$, and a fraction whose top is a multiple of
its bottom is a natural number.

## Check Yourself

1. Write $4 : 9$ as a fraction.
2. Write $\frac{15}{3}$ as a natural number.
3. Seven pizzas are shared equally among ten people. How much does each
   get?
4. Write $6$ as a fraction with denominator $1$. And with denominator
   $5$?
5. Is $\frac{45}{9}$ a natural number? Is $\frac{45}{6}$?
6. Someone writes "two bars among three friends: $\frac{3}{2}$ each."
   What's wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $\frac{4}{9}$.
> 2. $5$.
> 3. $\frac{7}{10}$ of a pizza.
> 4. $\frac{6}{1}$; $\frac{30}{5}$, since $30 : 5 = 6$.
> 5. Yes, $5$; no - $45 : 6$ leaves a remainder of $3$.
> 6. The dividend goes on top: $2 : 3 = \frac{2}{3}$, and each gets less
>    than one bar.
