## Why It Matters

A pack of stickers costs $12$ hryvnias and you want $7$ packs. You could
add $12$ seven times - or you could multiply once. A class of $24$ pupils
each bring $6$ books, a cinema hall has $18$ rows of $22$ seats, a heart
beats about $70$ times a minute for $60$ minutes - wherever the same
amount repeats, multiplication counts it all in one go.

## The Big Idea

Multiplication is repeated addition with a name. $12 \cdot 7$ is a short
way to write $12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 12$ - twelve taken seven times.
The best picture for it is a rectangle of dots: $7$ rows with $12$ dots in
each. Count the dots row by row and you're adding; know the answer at
once and you're multiplying. And here's the neat part: turn the rectangle
on its side, and it's $12$ rows of $7$ - the same dots, so the same total.
That's why the order of the factors never matters.

## The Rule

The numbers being multiplied are the **factors**, and the result is the
**product**:

$$
12 \cdot 7 = 84
$$

Here $12$ and $7$ are the factors and $84$ is the product. The dot $\cdot$
is the multiplication sign; the cross $\times$ means the same thing.

To multiply by a number of several digits in a column, multiply by each of
its digits separately, starting from the ones - each of these results is
shifted one place further to the left than the last - and then add them
up. Multiplying by a digit works like column addition: multiply from the
right, and carry whenever a product is $10$ or more.

Two special cases worth knowing by heart: $a \cdot 1 = a$ and
$a \cdot 0 = 0$. And to multiply by $10$, $100$, $1000$, just write that
many zeros after the number: $47 \cdot 100 = 4700$.

## Worked Example

Find $346 \cdot 27$.

- **Split the second factor by digits:** $27$ is $2$ tens and $7$ ones. So
  $346 \cdot 27$ is $346 \cdot 7$ plus $346 \cdot 20$.
- **Multiply by the ones digit,** $346 \cdot 7$: $6 \cdot 7 = 42$, write
  $2$, carry $4$; $4 \cdot 7 = 28$, plus $4$ is $32$, write $2$, carry $3$;
  $3 \cdot 7 = 21$, plus $3$ is $24$. Result: $2422$.
- **Multiply by the tens digit,** $346 \cdot 2 = 692$. But it's really
  $346 \cdot 20 = 6920$, so in the column this line is written one place
  to the left - under the tens, not the ones.
- **Add the two lines:** $2422 + 6920 = 9342$.

So $346 \cdot 27 = 9342$.

## Common Mistake

Not shifting the second line. When multiplying by the tens digit, the
result has to start under the tens column. Write $692$ directly under
$2422$ instead, and you're adding $346 \cdot 2$ rather than $346 \cdot 20$:
$2422 + 692 = 3114$, which is far too small. Every digit of the second
factor moves the line one more place to the left - ones, then tens, then
hundreds.

## The Takeaway

Multiplying is adding the same number many times, done all at once. In a
column, multiply by each digit of the second factor separately, shift each
line one place left, and add.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $58 \cdot 6$.
2. Find $217 \cdot 34$.
3. Write $9 + 9 + 9 + 9 + 9$ as a product and find it.
4. Find $305 \cdot 1000$ and $305 \cdot 0$.
5. A hall has $18$ rows of $22$ seats. How many seats is that?
6. Someone found $346 \cdot 27$ by adding $2422 + 692$ and got $3114$. What
   went wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $348$.
> 2. $7378$: $217 \cdot 4 = 868$, $217 \cdot 30 = 6510$, and
>    $868 + 6510 = 7378$.
> 3. $9 \cdot 5 = 45$.
> 4. $305\,000$ and $0$.
> 5. $18 \cdot 22 = 396$ seats.
> 6. The second line wasn't shifted: $346 \cdot 2$ is really
>    $346 \cdot 20 = 6920$, so the answer is $2422 + 6920 = 9342$.
