## Why It Matters

Nobody says "I ran eleven quarters of a kilometre." They say "two and
three-quarter kilometres" - and everyone instantly knows it's a bit less
than three. An improper fraction is exact but hard to picture; a mixed
number says the wholes first and the leftover part second, the way people
actually talk. Being able to switch between the two forms means you can
calculate in one and read the answer in the other.

## The Big Idea

$\frac{11}{4}$ is eleven quarters. Quarters come four to a whole, so
count out wholes: four quarters, eight quarters - that's two wholes, with
three quarters left over. Two and three-quarters. That counting-out is
division with a remainder: $11 : 4 = 2$ (remainder $3$). The quotient is
the whole part, the remainder is what's still a fraction, and the
denominator never changes - they're still quarters.

Going back is the same story reversed: two wholes are eight quarters,
plus the three loose ones makes eleven. Multiply the whole part by the
denominator, add the numerator.

## The Rule

A **mixed number** is a natural number and a proper fraction written side
by side, meaning their sum:

$$
2\frac{3}{4} = 2 + \frac{3}{4}
$$

The $2$ is its **whole part** and $\frac{3}{4}$ its **fractional part**.

**Improper fraction to mixed number:** divide the numerator by the
denominator with a remainder. The quotient is the whole part, the
remainder is the new numerator, the denominator stays:

$$
\frac{11}{4}: \quad 11 : 4 = 2 \text{ (remainder } 3) \quad \Rightarrow
\quad 2\frac{3}{4}
$$

If the remainder is $0$, the fraction is a natural number.

**Mixed number to improper fraction:** multiply the whole part by the
denominator, add the numerator, keep the denominator:

$$
2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \cdot 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}
$$

## Worked Example

Write $\frac{47}{6}$ as a mixed number.

- **Divide with a remainder:** $47 : 6$. $6 \cdot 7 = 42$, $6 \cdot 8 =
  48$ - too much. So $7$, remainder $47 - 42 = 5$.
- **Assemble:** whole part $7$, numerator $5$, denominator $6$:
  $7\frac{5}{6}$.
- **Check:** $7 \cdot 6 + 5 = 47$. Correct.

Now the other way: write $5\frac{2}{9}$ as an improper fraction. Five
wholes are $5 \cdot 9 = 45$ ninths; add the $2$: $47$ ninths.
$5\frac{2}{9} = \frac{47}{9}$.

## Common Mistake

Reading $2\frac{3}{4}$ as a product, $2 \cdot \frac{3}{4}$. The two parts
sit next to each other with no sign, and in algebra that would mean
multiply - but a mixed number is the one place where side by side means
*plus*. $2\frac{3}{4}$ is $2 + \frac{3}{4}$, nearly three; $2 \cdot
\frac{3}{4}$ is $\frac{6}{4}$, one and a half. Keep the two apart.

## The Takeaway

A mixed number is wholes plus a proper fraction. Improper to mixed is a
division with a remainder - quotient on the left, remainder on top.
Mixed to improper is whole part times denominator plus numerator, over
the same denominator.

## Check Yourself

1. Write $\frac{17}{5}$ as a mixed number.
2. Write $\frac{30}{6}$ as a mixed or natural number.
3. Write $3\frac{4}{7}$ as an improper fraction.
4. Write $10\frac{1}{2}$ as an improper fraction.
5. Which is bigger, $\frac{23}{8}$ or $3$? Convert first.
6. Someone turns $4\frac{1}{3}$ into $\frac{4 \cdot 1 + 3}{3} =
   \frac{7}{3}$. What went wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $17 : 5 = 3$ (remainder $2$): $3\frac{2}{5}$.
> 2. $30 : 6 = 5$ exactly: the natural number $5$.
> 3. $\frac{3 \cdot 7 + 4}{7} = \frac{25}{7}$.
> 4. $\frac{21}{2}$.
> 5. $\frac{23}{8} = 2\frac{7}{8} < 3$.
> 6. The whole part multiplies the denominator, not the numerator:
>    $\frac{4 \cdot 3 + 1}{3} = \frac{13}{3}$.
