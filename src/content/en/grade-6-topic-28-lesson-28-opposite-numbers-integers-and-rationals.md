## Why It Matters

Every step forward has a step back that undoes it; every deposit has a
withdrawal; every degree up has a degree down. Numbers come in such
pairs too - $5$ and $-5$ - and the pair is the key to subtracting
anything from anything. This topic also tidies up the zoo of numbers
you've met so far and gives the whole collection a name.

## The Big Idea

Fold the number line at zero. Every point lands on another point: $5$
lands on $-5$, $-2.5$ lands on $2.5$, $0$ lands on itself. Those two
points are opposites - same distance from the origin, opposite sides.
Put a minus sign in front of a number and you get its opposite;
put a minus in front of *that*, and you're back where you started.

With negatives added, the counting numbers grow into the integers:
$\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ - whole numbers in both directions.
Let the fractions in as well, positive and negative, and you have the
rational numbers: everything that can be written as a fraction with an
integer on top and a natural number on the bottom. Every number you've
ever met is one of those.

## The Rule

Two numbers are **opposite** if they differ only in sign: $5$ and $-5$,
$-\frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$. On the number line they're mirror images
across the origin. The opposite of $a$ is written $-a$, and the opposite
of the opposite is the number itself:

$$
-(-a) = a
$$

Zero is its own opposite.

The **integers** are the natural numbers, their opposites, and zero:

$$
\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots
$$

The **rational numbers** are all numbers of the form $\frac{m}{n}$, where
$m$ is an integer and $n$ is a natural number. They include all the
integers ($5 = \frac{5}{1}$), all the fractions and mixed numbers, and
all the decimals - terminating and repeating - with either sign.

## Worked Example

Write the opposites of $7$, $-3.5$, $\frac{1}{4}$ and $0$, and say which
of the following are integers: $-12$, $4.0$, $-2.5$, $\frac{9}{3}$,
$\frac{7}{2}$.

- **Opposites:** $-7$; $3.5$; $-\frac{1}{4}$; $0$.
- **$-12$:** an integer - a negative whole number.
- **$4.0$:** that's just $4$ - an integer.
- **$-2.5$:** not an integer, it's between $-3$ and $-2$; but rational,
  $-\frac{5}{2}$.
- **$\frac{9}{3} = 3$:** an integer in disguise.
- **$\frac{7}{2} = 3.5$:** not an integer; rational.

Simplify $-(-(-4))$: the inside $-(-4)$ is $4$, and then $-4$. An odd
number of minus signs leaves a minus.

## Common Mistake

Thinking $-a$ is always negative. If $a = -6$, then $-a = -(-6) = 6$ - a
positive number. The minus in front of a letter means "the opposite
of," not "a negative number"; which sign $-a$ has depends on what $a$
is. Read $-a$ as "the opposite of $a$" and the confusion disappears.

## The Takeaway

Opposite numbers are mirror images across zero, and the opposite of the
opposite is the original. Integers are whole numbers with either sign;
rationals are everything writable as a fraction - every number you know
so far.

## Check Yourself

1. Write the opposite of $-9$, of $0.75$, and of $-\frac{5}{8}$.
2. Simplify $-(-13)$ and $-(-(-2))$.
3. Which of these are integers: $-7$, $\frac{10}{5}$, $-0.5$, $0$,
   $3\frac{1}{2}$?
4. Which of the same numbers are rational?
5. If $-a = 4$, what is $a$?
6. Someone says "$-x$ is a negative number." When is that false?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $9$; $-0.75$; $\frac{5}{8}$.
> 2. $13$; $-2$.
> 3. $-7$, $\frac{10}{5} = 2$, and $0$.
> 4. All of them.
> 5. $a = -4$.
> 6. Whenever $x$ is negative - then $-x$ is positive; and $-0 = 0$ is
>    neither.
