## Why It Matters

A $120$-km trip: at speed $v$ it takes $\frac{120}{v}$ hours. Double the
speed, halve the time - inverse proportionality, from grade 6. Now
it's a function, $y = \frac{k}{x}$, and it has a graph unlike any
you've drawn: two curved branches that sweep toward the axes and
never touch them. The hyperbola is the shape of every "more of this,
less of that" trade-off - pressure and volume, price and demand, speed
and time.

## The Big Idea

$y = \frac{k}{x}$ with $k > 0$: when $x$ is tiny, $y$ is huge; when $x$ is
huge, $y$ is tiny; and $x$ can't be zero at all. Plot points for $k = 6$ -
$(1; 6)$, $(2; 3)$, $(3; 2)$, $(6; 1)$ - and they trace a curve falling
toward the $x$-axis as it goes right and climbing toward the $y$-axis
as it goes left, in the first quadrant. Negative $x$ gives negative $y$,
a mirror-image branch in the third quadrant. Neither branch ever
reaches an axis: $y$ is never zero, and $x$ is never zero.

For $k < 0$ the signs flip and the branches sit in quadrants II and IV.
In both cases the graph is symmetric about the origin - $(x; y)$ on it
means $(-x; -y)$ is too.

## The Rule

The function $y = \frac{k}{x}$, $k \neq 0$, has:

- **domain:** all $x \neq 0$; **range:** all $y \neq 0$;
- **graph:** a **hyperbola** - two branches;
- for $k > 0$: branches in quadrants $I$ and $III$, the function
  decreasing on each branch;
- for $k < 0$: branches in quadrants $II$ and $IV$, the function
  increasing on each branch;
- symmetry about the origin: $(x; y)$ on the graph implies $(-x; -y)$
  is;
- the axes are **asymptotes**: the branches approach them without
  touching.

It's the graph of inverse proportionality: $xy = k$ at every point.

## Worked Example

Plot $y = \frac{6}{x}$ and read from it: $y$ at $x = 1.5$, and $x$ when
$y = -4$. Then decide whether $(3; -2)$ lies on $y = -\frac{6}{x}$.

- **Table for $k = 6$:** $x = 1, 2, 3, 6$ gives $y = 6, 3, 2, 1$; and $x =
  -1, -2, -3, -6$ gives $y = -6, -3, -2, -1$. Two branches, quadrants $I$
  and $III$.
- **$x = 1.5$:** $y = 6 : 1.5 = 4$. On the graph, up from $1.5$ to the
  right branch, across to $4$.
- **$y = -4$:** $x = 6 : (-4) = -1.5$. The left branch.
- **$(3; -2)$ on $y = -\frac{6}{x}$?** $-\frac{6}{3} = -2$. Yes; this graph
  has branches in quadrants $II$ and $IV$.

The trip: $t = \frac{120}{v}$; at $v = 60$, $2$ h; at $v = 120$, $1$ h - the
$k = 120$ hyperbola in the first quadrant.

## Common Mistake

Joining the two branches across the origin, or letting a branch touch
an axis. There's no point with $x = 0$, so the graph has a gap there -
two separate pieces. And $y = \frac{k}{x}$ is never zero, so the branch
approaching the $x$-axis flattens forever without arriving. Draw the
branches as separate curves that hug the axes.

## The Takeaway

$y = \frac{k}{x}$ is inverse proportionality as a function: a hyperbola
with two branches, in quadrants $I$ and $III$ for $k > 0$ and $II$ and
$IV$ for $k < 0$, symmetric about the origin, never touching the axes.
Every point satisfies $xy = k$.

## Check Yourself

1. What are the domain and range of $y = \frac{5}{x}$?
2. In which quadrants is the graph of $y = -\frac{3}{x}$?
3. Does $(-2; -4)$ lie on $y = \frac{8}{x}$?
4. Find $k$ if the graph of $y = \frac{k}{x}$ passes through $(4; -1.5)$.
5. On $y = \frac{6}{x}$, what happens to $y$ as $x$ grows without bound?
6. Someone draws $y = \frac{2}{x}$ as one continuous curve through the
   origin. What's wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $x \neq 0$; $y \neq 0$.
> 2. $II$ and $IV$.
> 3. Yes: $\frac{8}{-2} = -4$.
> 4. $k = 4 \cdot (-1.5) = -6$.
> 5. It approaches $0$ but never reaches it.
> 6. $x = 0$ is not in the domain; the graph is two separate branches.
