## Why It Matters

You've done $\frac{1}{3}$ of your homework before dinner and $\frac{2}{5}$
after - how much is done? A recipe needs $\frac{3}{4}$ of a cup of milk
and you have $\frac{1}{2}$ - how much is missing? In grade 5 you could
only add fractions cut the same way. With common denominators in hand,
you can now add any two fractions at all - which is what most fraction
arithmetic in real life needs.

## The Big Idea

Thirds and fifths can't be added as they stand, for the same reason $3$
apples and $2$ pears aren't "$5$ apples." First re-cut both into the
same slice size - fifteenths - and then it's grade-5 addition: $5$
fifteenths plus $6$ fifteenths is $11$ fifteenths. The common
denominator is the whole of the new work; the adding itself is the old
rule.

## The Rule

To add or subtract fractions with different denominators:

1. bring them to the least common denominator;
2. add or subtract the numerators, keeping the denominator;
3. cancel the result if possible, and turn an improper fraction into a
   mixed number if that reads better.

$$
\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{5}{15} + \frac{6}{15} = \frac{11}{15}
$$

A natural number joins in as a fraction over $1$, or as "denominator
over denominator" for one whole.

## Worked Example

Find $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{3}{8}$.

- **Least common denominator:** LCM of $6$ and $8$ is $24$.
- **Additional factors:** $24 : 6 = 4$ and $24 : 8 = 3$.
- **Rewrite:** $\frac{20}{24} - \frac{9}{24}$.
- **Subtract the numerators:** $\frac{11}{24}$.
- **Cancel?** $11$ and $24$ are coprime - it's irreducible.

Now the homework: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{11}{15}$. And the
milk: $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$
of a cup is missing.

One more: $\frac{7}{10} + \frac{4}{15}$. LCM is $30$: $\frac{21}{30} +
\frac{8}{30} = \frac{29}{30}$.

## Common Mistake

Adding tops and bottoms separately: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} =
\frac{3}{8}$. But $\frac{3}{8}$ is less than $\frac{2}{5}$ alone, and adding
$\frac{1}{3}$ to something can't make it smaller. Denominators are slice
sizes; they don't add. Re-cut first, then add the counts.

## The Takeaway

Different denominators mean different slice sizes: bring the fractions
to their least common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators
only. Cancel at the end.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{2}{3}$.
2. Find $\frac{7}{9} - \frac{1}{6}$.
3. Find $\frac{3}{10} + \frac{5}{6}$ and write it as a mixed number.
4. Find $1 - \frac{5}{12} - \frac{1}{4}$.
5. A pole is $\frac{2}{7}$ in the ground and $\frac{1}{2}$ in the water.
   What fraction is above the water?
6. Someone finds $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{8}$. Why is that
   impossible?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $\frac{3}{12} + \frac{8}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$.
> 2. $\frac{14}{18} - \frac{3}{18} = \frac{11}{18}$.
> 3. $\frac{9}{30} + \frac{25}{30} = \frac{34}{30} = 1\frac{4}{30} =
>    1\frac{2}{15}$.
> 4. $\frac{12}{12} - \frac{5}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{4}{12} =
>    \frac{1}{3}$.
> 5. $1 - \frac{4}{14} - \frac{7}{14} = \frac{3}{14}$.
> 6. $\frac{3}{8} < \frac{2}{5}$, and a sum can't be less than one of its
>    terms; the answer is $\frac{11}{15}$.
