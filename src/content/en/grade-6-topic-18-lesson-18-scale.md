## Why It Matters

A map fits a whole country on a sheet of paper. A floor plan fits a
flat on a screen. A drawing of a bacterium is a thousand times bigger
than the bacterium. Each of these shrinks or enlarges everything by the
same factor, and that factor is written in a corner as the scale. Read
it right and a ruler on the map measures kilometres on the ground.

## The Big Idea

A map at $1 : 100\,000$ says: one unit on the map is $100\,000$ of the same
units in reality. One centimetre on the paper is $100\,000$ cm - one
kilometre - on the ground. Every distance on the map is one hundred
thousandth of the real one, so map distance and real distance are
directly proportional, and the scale is their ratio.

Because it's a ratio, it works both ways: map to ground by multiplying,
ground to map by dividing. And because both distances are in the same
units when you write the ratio, the units have to be converted before
anything else.

## The Rule

The **scale** of a map, plan or drawing is the ratio of a length on it
to the corresponding real length:

$$
\text{scale} = \text{length on the map} : \text{real length}
$$

written with the same units on both sides, usually as $1 : n$.

- Real length $=$ map length $\cdot n$.
- Map length $=$ real length $: n$.

A scale $1 : n$ with $n > 1$ *reduces*; a scale like $10 : 1$ *enlarges* -
drawings of small things use those.

Any distance problem on a map is a proportion: $\frac{\text{map}}{\text{real}}
= \frac{1}{n}$.

## Worked Example

On a map at $1 : 500\,000$ two towns are $4.6$ cm apart. What's the real
distance?

- **Multiply by $n$:** $4.6 \cdot 500\,000 = 2\,300\,000$ cm.
- **Convert to sensible units:** $2\,300\,000$ cm $= 23\,000$ m $= 23$ km.

The towns are $23$ km apart.

Now the other way. A road is $36$ km long. How long is it on a map at
$1 : 200\,000$? Convert first: $36$ km $= 3\,600\,000$ cm. Divide by $n$:
$3\,600\,000 : 200\,000 = 18$ cm.

And finding the scale: a $150$-m-long football pitch is $7.5$ cm on a
plan. $7.5$ cm : $15\,000$ cm $= 1 : 2000$.

## Common Mistake

Skipping the unit conversion. $4.6$ cm on the map times $500\,000$ is
$2\,300\,000$ - but $2\,300\,000$ *centimetres*, not kilometres or metres.
The scale ratio is unit-free only because both lengths are in the same
unit; multiply in centimetres, then convert. Forgetting this produces
distances a hundred thousand times too big.

## The Takeaway

Scale is the ratio of map length to real length, in the same units.
Multiply by $n$ to go from map to ground, divide by $n$ to go from ground
to map - and convert units before and after.

## Check Yourself

1. On a $1 : 50\,000$ map, a lake is $3$ cm long. How long is it really?
2. A river is $12$ km long. How long is it on a $1 : 400\,000$ map?
3. A room $6$ m by $4$ m is drawn at $1 : 100$. What are its dimensions
   on the plan?
4. Two cities $180$ km apart are $9$ cm apart on a map. Find the scale.
5. A drawing of an ant at $20 : 1$ shows it $8$ cm long. How long is the
   ant?
6. Someone says two towns $5$ cm apart on a $1 : 100\,000$ map are
   $500\,000$ km apart. Where's the mistake?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $150\,000$ cm $= 1.5$ km.
> 2. $1\,200\,000 : 400\,000 = 3$ cm.
> 3. $6$ cm by $4$ cm.
> 4. $9 : 18\,000\,000 = 1 : 2\,000\,000$.
> 5. $8 : 20 = 0.4$ cm $= 4$ mm.
> 6. The units - $500\,000$ cm is $5$ km.
