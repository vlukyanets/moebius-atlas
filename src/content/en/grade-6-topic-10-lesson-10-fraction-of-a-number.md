## Why It Matters

$\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour - how many minutes? $\frac{2}{5}$ of the $240$
pages - how many read? $\frac{7}{10}$ of a $6.5$-litre tank - how much
petrol? In grade 5 you found a fraction of a number by dividing and
multiplying in two steps. Now that fractions can be multiplied, it's one
step - and it works for any numbers, including fractions of fractions
and fractions of decimals.

## The Big Idea

"Three quarters of $60$" is three quarters *times* $60$. The grade-5
recipe - divide by the denominator, multiply by the numerator - is
exactly what multiplying by $\frac{3}{4}$ does: $60 \cdot \frac{3}{4} =
\frac{60 \cdot 3}{4}$, and dividing by $4$ then multiplying by $3$ is
the same as multiplying by $3$ then dividing by $4$. The word "of"
between a fraction and a number is a multiplication sign in disguise.

## The Rule

To find a fraction of a number, **multiply the number by the fraction**:

$$
\frac{a}{b} \text{ of } c = c \cdot \frac{a}{b}
$$

The number $c$ can be natural, a fraction, a mixed number or a decimal.
Percents are the same rule with a hundredth: $p\%$ of $c$ is
$c \cdot \frac{p}{100}$.

## Worked Example

A tank holds $6.5$ litres and is $\frac{7}{10}$ full. How much petrol is
in it?

- **Write "of" as a multiplication:** $6.5 \cdot \frac{7}{10}$.
- **Multiply:** $\frac{7}{10} = 0.7$, so $6.5 \cdot 0.7 = 4.55$ litres. Or
  as fractions, $\frac{13}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{10} = \frac{91}{20} = 4.55$.

Now a fraction of a fraction: $\frac{2}{3}$ of $\frac{9}{10}$ of a cake.
$\frac{9}{10} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{5}$ of the cake.

And the pages: $\frac{2}{5}$ of $240$ is $240 \cdot \frac{2}{5} = 96$.

## Common Mistake

Dividing instead of multiplying because the fraction "looks like a
division." $\frac{3}{4}$ of $60$ is $60 \cdot \frac{3}{4} = 45$, not
$60 : \frac{3}{4} = 80$. A proper fraction of a number is *less* than the
number - a size check tells you which operation you needed. Dividing by
a fraction is for the reverse question, finding the number from its
part.

## The Takeaway

A fraction of a number is the number times the fraction - "of" means
multiply. It works for any kind of number, and percents are the same
thing with denominator $100$.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $\frac{3}{4}$ of $60$ minutes.
2. Find $\frac{5}{8}$ of $\frac{4}{15}$.
3. Find $\frac{2}{3}$ of $4.2$.
4. Find $\frac{3}{7}$ of $2\frac{1}{3}$.
5. A shop sold $\frac{5}{12}$ of its $360$ ice creams before noon. How
   many were sold before noon, and how many after?
6. Someone finds $\frac{3}{4}$ of $60$ as $80$. Why is that impossible?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $45$ minutes.
> 2. $\frac{5}{8} \cdot \frac{4}{15} = \frac{1}{6}$.
> 3. $2.8$.
> 4. $\frac{7}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{7} = 1$.
> 5. $150$ before, $210$ after.
> 6. Three quarters of something is less than the whole $60$; the answer
>    is $45$.
