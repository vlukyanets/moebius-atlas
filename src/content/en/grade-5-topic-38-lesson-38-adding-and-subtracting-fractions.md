## Why It Matters

You ate $\frac{3}{8}$ of the pizza and your friend ate $\frac{2}{8}$ - how
much is gone? How much is left? A recipe wants $\frac{3}{4}$ of a cup of
flour and you've put in $\frac{1}{4}$ - how much more? Adding and taking
away parts of a whole is everyday arithmetic, and when the parts are the
same size it's the easiest arithmetic there is.

## The Big Idea

Three apples plus two apples is five apples - you add the counts, and
"apples" stays "apples." Three eighths plus two eighths is five eighths
for exactly the same reason: eighths are the kind of thing, and you're
just counting how many. The denominator is a *name*, and names don't
add. Only the numerators - the counts - do.

Subtraction is the same: five eighths take away two eighths leaves three
eighths. And if the count ever reaches the denominator, you've got a
whole: eight eighths is one pizza.

## The Rule

To add fractions with the same denominator, add the numerators and keep
the denominator:

$$
\frac{a}{c} + \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a + b}{c}
$$

To subtract, subtract the numerators and keep the denominator:

$$
\frac{a}{c} - \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a - b}{c}
$$

If the result is improper, it can be written as a mixed number; if it
has numerator $0$, it's $0$.

A natural number can join in by writing it over the same denominator:
$1 = \frac{8}{8}$, so $1 - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{8}{8} - \frac{3}{8} =
\frac{5}{8}$.

## Worked Example

Find $\frac{5}{12} + \frac{11}{12}$ and write the answer as a mixed
number.

- **Same denominator** - twelfths - so add the counts: $5 + 11 = 16$.
- **Keep the denominator:** $\frac{16}{12}$.
- **It's improper** ($16 > 12$), so convert: $16 : 12 = 1$ (remainder
  $4$), giving $1\frac{4}{12}$.

So $\frac{5}{12} + \frac{11}{12} = 1\frac{4}{12}$.

Now the pizza. Gone: $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$. Left: the
whole pizza is $\frac{8}{8}$, so $\frac{8}{8} - \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{8}$.

And the flour: $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{4}$ of a cup more.

## Common Mistake

Adding the denominators too. $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{8}$ is not
$\frac{5}{16}$. Sixteenths are *smaller* pieces than eighths, so
$\frac{5}{16}$ is less than $\frac{3}{8}$ alone - adding something can't
make the total smaller. Three eighths and two eighths are five of the
same thing, eighths. The denominator is the name of the piece and stays
put.

## The Takeaway

Same denominator, same-sized pieces: add or subtract the numerators and
leave the denominator alone. Turn an improper answer into a mixed number
if it reads better, and write $1$ as "denominator over denominator" when
a whole joins in.

## Check Yourself

1. Find $\frac{4}{9} + \frac{2}{9}$.
2. Find $\frac{13}{15} - \frac{7}{15}$.
3. Find $\frac{7}{10} + \frac{9}{10}$ as a mixed number.
4. Find $1 - \frac{5}{11}$.
5. A tank was $\frac{7}{20}$ full and $\frac{9}{20}$ more was added. How
   full is it now, and how much room is left?
6. Someone finds $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{8} = \frac{5}{16}$. Why is that
   impossible?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $\frac{6}{9}$.
> 2. $\frac{6}{15}$.
> 3. $\frac{16}{10} = 1\frac{6}{10}$.
> 4. $\frac{11}{11} - \frac{5}{11} = \frac{6}{11}$.
> 5. $\frac{16}{20}$ full; $\frac{20}{20} - \frac{16}{20} = \frac{4}{20}$
>    of room.
> 6. $\frac{5}{16}$ is smaller than $\frac{3}{8}$ ($= \frac{6}{16}$), and
>    adding can't shrink a number; the answer is $\frac{5}{8}$.
