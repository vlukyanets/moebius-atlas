## Why It Matters

$(-0.25) \cdot 7 \cdot (-4)$ is two sign decisions and a decimal
multiplication - or it's $(-0.25) \cdot (-4) = 1$, times $7$, in your
head. And an expression like $2 \cdot a \cdot (-3) \cdot b$ is much
easier to read as $-6ab$. The properties of multiplication survive the
arrival of negatives, and they're what turns a tangle of factors into
one clean number in front of the letters.

## The Big Idea

Multiplication was always free to rearrange: swap the factors, group
them any way. Negatives don't change that - the product of several
signed numbers has a fixed size and a fixed sign no matter what order
you multiply in. So in a long product, collect the numbers together
and multiply them first, hunting for pairs that give $1$, $10$ or $100$,
and let the sign come from counting the minuses.

Do that to $2 \cdot a \cdot (-3) \cdot b$: the numbers give $2 \cdot (-3)
= -6$, the letters give $ab$, and the whole thing is $-6ab$. That number
in front - $-6$ - is the coefficient. It says how many of $ab$ you've
got.

## The Rule

For any rational numbers $a$, $b$, $c$:

- **Commutative:** $a \cdot b = b \cdot a$.
- **Associative:** $(a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$.

So in a product of several factors, the numerical factors can be
gathered together and multiplied first, and the letters written after
them in alphabetical order.

The **coefficient** of an expression that is a product of numbers and
letters is the numerical factor in front, once the numbers are
multiplied out: in $-6ab$ the coefficient is $-6$. When no number is
written, the coefficient is $1$: $ab$ means $1 \cdot ab$. And $-ab$ has
coefficient $-1$.

## Worked Example

Simplify $2 \cdot a \cdot (-3) \cdot b$ and $-0.5x \cdot (-8) \cdot y$, and
find $(-0.25) \cdot 7 \cdot (-4)$.

- **$2 \cdot a \cdot (-3) \cdot b$.** Gather the numbers: $2 \cdot (-3) =
  -6$. Gather the letters: $ab$. Result $-6ab$, coefficient $-6$.
- **$-0.5x \cdot (-8) \cdot y$.** Numbers: $(-0.5) \cdot (-8) = 4$. Letters:
  $xy$. Result $4xy$, coefficient $4$.
- **$(-0.25) \cdot 7 \cdot (-4)$.** Regroup: $(-0.25) \cdot (-4) = 1$, then
  $1 \cdot 7 = 7$.

Name the coefficients of $-x$, $\frac{2}{3}mn$ and $abc$: $-1$,
$\frac{2}{3}$ and $1$.

## Common Mistake

Losing a sign while regrouping, or reading $-x$ as having no
coefficient. Rearranging $2 \cdot a \cdot (-3) \cdot b$ as $2 \cdot 3 \cdot
ab = 6ab$ dropped the minus that belonged to the $3$. Each factor
travels with its sign. And $-x$ is not "coefficient-less" - it's
$(-1) \cdot x$, coefficient $-1$; forgetting that breaks collecting
like terms later.

## The Takeaway

Factors can be swapped and regrouped even with signs: multiply the
numbers first, letters after, and the sign follows the count of
minuses. The number left in front is the coefficient - $1$ or $-1$ when
nothing is written.

## Check Yourself

1. Simplify $4 \cdot x \cdot (-5) \cdot y$.
2. Simplify $(-3a) \cdot (-2b) \cdot c$.
3. Find $(-2.5) \cdot 13 \cdot (-4)$ in the easiest way.
4. Name the coefficients of $-7ab$, $\frac{x}{2}$ and $-m$.
5. Simplify $-1.5 \cdot m \cdot 2 \cdot (-n) \cdot (-1)$.
6. Someone simplifies $6 \cdot p \cdot (-2) \cdot q$ to $12pq$. What's
   the right answer?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $-20xy$.
> 2. $6abc$.
> 3. $((-2.5) \cdot (-4)) \cdot 13 = 10 \cdot 13 = 130$.
> 4. $-7$; $\frac{1}{2}$; $-1$.
> 5. Numbers $(-1.5) \cdot 2 \cdot (-1) \cdot (-1) = -3$: $-3mn$.
> 6. $-12pq$ - the minus on the $2$ was dropped.
