## Why It Matters

"I'm thinking of a number. If you add $17$ to it, you get $45$. What's my
number?" You've solved puzzles like this since you were small - by
thinking backwards. An equation is that puzzle written in symbols:
$x + 17 = 45$. Once you can write a question as an equation and solve it,
you have a tool that works for every "find the unknown" problem there is -
from splitting a bill to designing a bridge.

## The Big Idea

Picture a balance scale, level. On the left pan sit a mystery box and $17$
one-gram weights; on the right, $45$ weights. Because the pans balance,
the box plus $17$ weighs the same as $45$. To find the box, take $17$
weights off the left pan - but then you must take $17$ off the right too,
or the scale tips. What's left is the box alone on one side and $28$
weights on the other. The box weighs $28$.

That's all solving an equation is: keeping the two sides equal while you
strip away everything that isn't the unknown.

## The Rule

An **equation** is an equality that contains an unknown number, written
as a letter: $x + 17 = 45$. The **root** of the equation is the value of
the letter that makes the equality true; **to solve** an equation is to
find all its roots, or to show there are none.

In grade 5 every equation is undone by reading an operation backwards -
the unknown is one part of an addition, subtraction, multiplication or
division:

- unknown term: $x + 17 = 45 \Rightarrow x = 45 - 17$;
- unknown minuend: $x - 9 = 20 \Rightarrow x = 20 + 9$;
- unknown subtrahend: $30 - x = 12 \Rightarrow x = 30 - 12$;
- unknown factor: $6x = 42 \Rightarrow x = 42 : 6$;
- unknown dividend: $x : 5 = 8 \Rightarrow x = 8 \cdot 5$;
- unknown divisor: $56 : x = 7 \Rightarrow x = 56 : 7$.

Always **check**: substitute the root back into the original equation and
see that both sides come out the same.

## Worked Example

Solve $3x + 8 = 29$.

- **Look at the outermost operation.** The left side is a sum: something
  ($3x$) plus $8$. So $3x$ is an unknown term.
- **Undo the addition:** $3x = 29 - 8$, so $3x = 21$.
- **Now $x$ is an unknown factor:** $x = 21 : 3$, so $x = 7$.
- **Check:** $3 \cdot 7 + 8 = 21 + 8 = 29$. Both sides match.

The root is $7$.

Now the puzzle, written as an equation: $x + 17 = 45$. The unknown is a
term, so $x = 45 - 17 = 28$. Check: $28 + 17 = 45$.

## Common Mistake

Undoing the operations in the wrong order. In $3x + 8 = 29$, some people
divide by $3$ first and get $x + 8 = 29 : 3$ - which isn't even a whole
number. The $3$ multiplies only $x$, not the $8$, so the addition of $8$
is the outer layer and must be peeled off first. Ask "what was done to
$x$ last?" - and undo that first.

## The Takeaway

An equation is an equality with an unknown, and solving it is undoing the
operations around the unknown, outermost first, keeping both sides equal
at every step. Substituting the root back in is how you know you're done.

## Check Yourself

1. Solve $x - 26 = 54$.
2. Solve $7x = 91$.
3. Solve $84 : x = 12$.
4. Solve $4x - 15 = 33$ and check.
5. Is $9$ a root of $5x + 4 = 49$? Show how you know.
6. Write as an equation and solve: "a number was multiplied by $8$, and
   the result is $120$."

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $x = 54 + 26 = 80$.
> 2. $x = 91 : 7 = 13$.
> 3. $x = 84 : 12 = 7$.
> 4. $4x = 48$, $x = 12$; check: $4 \cdot 12 - 15 = 33$.
> 5. Yes: $5 \cdot 9 + 4 = 49$, so both sides match.
> 6. $8x = 120$, so $x = 120 : 8 = 15$.
