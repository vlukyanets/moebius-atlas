## Why It Matters

A shop says "you save $240$ hryvnias - that's $20\%$ off!" - so what did
the jacket cost before? A test says "you got $42$ right, that's $70\%$" -
how many questions were there? Yesterday's topic went from the whole to
a part; today goes the other way, from a known part and its percent
back to the whole. It's the same relationship read backwards.

## The Big Idea

If $20\%$ of a price is $240$ hryvnias, then one percent is $240 : 20 =
12$ hryvnias, and the whole - a hundred percent - is $12 \cdot 100 = 1200$.
That's the entire idea: from the part, work out what one percent is
worth, then scale up to a hundred.

Written as one step, it's a division: the part divided by the percent as
a decimal. $240 : 0.2 = 1200$. Since $p\%$ of $a$ is $a \cdot \frac{p}{100}$,
finding $a$ from that product is undoing a multiplication - dividing.

## The Rule

If $p\%$ of an unknown number is $b$, the number is

$$
a = b : \frac{p}{100}
$$

- divide the known part by the percent written as a decimal. Or in two
  steps: divide by $p$ to get $1\%$, then multiply by $100$.

The check is the previous topic: $p\%$ of the answer must give $b$ back.

## Worked Example

$70\%$ of the questions on a test is $42$ questions. How many questions
were there?

- **Percent to decimal:** $70\% = 0.7$.
- **Divide the part by it:** $42 : 0.7 = 420 : 7 = 60$.
- **Check:** $70\%$ of $60$ is $60 \cdot 0.7 = 42$. Correct.

There were $60$ questions.

Now in two steps. A deposit grew by $8\%$, which was $400$ hryvnias. One
percent is $400 : 8 = 50$, so the deposit was $50 \cdot 100 = 5000$
hryvnias.

## Common Mistake

Multiplying when you should divide. $42$ is $70\%$ of *something*, and
that something must be bigger than $42$ - the whole is more than a
part. Computing $42 \cdot 0.7 = 29.4$ gives a "whole" smaller than its
own part, which is impossible. From part to whole is a division; from
whole to part is a multiplication. A size check tells you which you
needed.

## The Takeaway

Knowing a part and what percent it is, divide the part by the percent as
a decimal to get the whole - or find $1\%$ first and multiply by $100$.
The whole is always bigger than any part below $100\%$.

## Check Yourself

1. $25\%$ of a number is $15$. Find the number.
2. $12\%$ of a number is $36$. Find the number.
3. A discount of $180$ hryvnias was $15\%$ of the price. What was the
   price?
4. $45$ pupils is $30\%$ of a school year. How many pupils are in the
   year?
5. A shirt costs $560$ hryvnias after a $30\%$ discount. What was the old
   price? (Careful: $560$ is $70\%$, not $30\%$.)
6. Someone finds the number whose $70\%$ is $42$ as $42 \cdot 0.7 =
   29.4$. Why is that impossible?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $15 : 0.25 = 60$.
> 2. $36 : 0.12 = 300$.
> 3. $180 : 0.15 = 1200$ hryvnias.
> 4. $45 : 0.3 = 150$.
> 5. $560 : 0.7 = 800$ hryvnias.
> 6. The whole can't be smaller than its $70\%$; it's $42 : 0.7 = 60$.
