## Why It Matters

Twice as many notebooks, twice the bill. Three times as long on the
road at a steady speed, three times the distance. Half the flour, half
the cake. Some pairs of quantities move together in lockstep - scale one
and the other scales by the same factor - and that relationship has a
name. Spotting it is what tells you a problem can be solved with a
proportion at all.

## The Big Idea

A notebook costs $15$ hryvnias. One costs $15$, two cost $30$, five cost
$75$: the cost is always the number of notebooks *times* $15$. The
ratio of cost to number never changes - it's $15$ every time. That
fixed ratio is the whole story: two quantities are directly
proportional when their ratio stays constant, so that doubling one
doubles the other and halving one halves the other.

Not every "more gives more" pair works this way. A taxi with a $40$
hryvnia flag fall charges $52$ for one kilometre and $64$ for two - not
double. The ratio changes, so it isn't proportionality.

## The Rule

Two quantities are **directly proportional** if, when one of them is
multiplied by some number, the other is multiplied by the same number.
Equivalently, their ratio is a constant:

$$
\frac{y}{x} = k, \qquad \text{or} \qquad y = k \cdot x
$$

The number $k$ is the **coefficient of proportionality** - here, the
price of one notebook.

For directly proportional quantities, any two pairs of values form a
proportion:

$$
\frac{y_1}{y_2} = \frac{x_1}{x_2}
$$

That is what lets you solve "if $x_1$ gives $y_1$, what does $x_2$ give?"
by cross-multiplying.

## Worked Example

A car at a steady speed covers $180$ km in $3$ hours. How far does it
go in $5$ hours?

- **Check it's direct proportionality.** Steady speed: more time, more
  distance, by the same factor. Distance and time are directly
  proportional; the coefficient is the speed.
- **Set up the proportion:** $\frac{180}{x} = \frac{3}{5}$ - distances
  on top, times on the bottom, in the same order.
- **Cross-multiply:** $3x = 180 \cdot 5 = 900$, so $x = 300$.

The car goes $300$ km. Or via the coefficient: $k = 180 : 3 = 60$ km/h,
and $60 \cdot 5 = 300$.

Now a non-example. $2$ workers dig a trench in $6$ hours. Do $4$ workers
take $12$? No - more workers means *less* time. That's not direct
proportionality (it's the inverse kind, coming soon).

## Common Mistake

Assuming every increase is proportional. "One pizza feeds $3$ people,
so how many does a pizza with twice the diameter feed?" is not $6$ - the
area, and the amount of pizza, is four times as big. And the taxi
fare with a fixed starting charge doesn't double when the distance
does. Before setting up a proportion, ask: if I double this, does that
*exactly* double? If not, a proportion is the wrong tool.

## The Takeaway

Direct proportionality means a constant ratio: $y = kx$, and scaling one
quantity scales the other by the same factor. Two pairs of values then
make a proportion you can solve by cross-multiplying - but only after
checking the relationship really is proportional.

## Check Yourself

1. $5$ kg of apples cost $150$ hryvnias. What do $8$ kg cost?
2. A printer prints $45$ pages in $3$ minutes. How many in $7$ minutes?
3. Are these directly proportional: the side of a square and its
   perimeter? The side of a square and its area?
4. $y$ is directly proportional to $x$ with $k = 4$. Find $y$ when
   $x = 2.5$, and $x$ when $y = 30$.
5. A map scale gives $1$ cm $= 5$ km. Is map distance directly
   proportional to real distance? What's $k$?
6. Someone says a $12$-year-old is twice as tall as a $6$-year-old,
   "since age and height are proportional." Why is that wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $150 : 5 \cdot 8 = 240$ hryvnias.
> 2. $45 : 3 \cdot 7 = 105$ pages.
> 3. Perimeter yes ($P = 4a$); area no (doubling the side quadruples the
>    area).
> 4. $y = 10$; $x = 7.5$.
> 5. Yes; $k = 5$ km per cm.
> 6. Height isn't a constant times age - a newborn isn't $0$ cm tall, and
>    growth isn't steady. Not proportional.
