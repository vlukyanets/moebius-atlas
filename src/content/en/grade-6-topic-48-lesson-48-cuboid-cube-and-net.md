## Why It Matters

A shoebox, a brick, a room, a fridge, a die, a shipping container. The
most common solid shape people make is a box with six flat rectangular
faces - and the questions about it are practical: how much cardboard to
make one, how much paint to cover it, how to cut it out flat so it
folds up right. This is the first time the atlas leaves the flat page
and picks up a solid object.

## The Big Idea

A cuboid is what you get by stacking rectangles: take a rectangle,
lift it straight up, and everything it sweeps through is the solid. So
its top and bottom are that rectangle, and its four walls are
rectangles too - six faces in all, meeting at twelve edges and eight
corners. Three numbers describe it completely: how long, how wide, how
tall. A cube is the cuboid with all three the same.

Cut a box along seven of its edges and unfold it flat, and you get its
net - the six rectangles laid out edge to edge. Fold the net back and
the box reassembles. The net is how a box is made from a sheet, and its
area is how much sheet the box needs.

## The Rule

A **cuboid** (rectangular parallelepiped) is a solid bounded by six
rectangles, its **faces**. Faces meet along $12$ **edges**, and edges
meet at $8$ **vertices**. Opposite faces are equal rectangles. The
three edges meeting at one vertex are its **dimensions**: length $a$,
width $b$, height $c$.

A **cube** is a cuboid with all edges equal: every face is a square.

A **net** of a cuboid is a flat figure made of its six faces joined
along edges, which folds up into the cuboid. The **surface area** is
the total area of the six faces - the area of the net:

$$
S = 2(ab + bc + ac)
$$

For a cube with edge $a$: $S = 6a^2$.

## Worked Example

A box is $40$ cm long, $30$ cm wide and $20$ cm high. How much cardboard
is its surface? Then: a cube has edge $5$ cm - find its surface area.

- **Three pairs of faces.** Top and bottom: $40 \times 30$, area $1200$
  each. Front and back: $40 \times 20$, area $800$ each. Sides: $30 \times
  20$, area $600$ each.
- **Add:** $2 \cdot (1200 + 800 + 600) = 2 \cdot 2600 = 5200\
  \text{cm}^2$.
- **The cube:** six squares of $5 \times 5$: $6 \cdot 25 = 150\ \text{cm}^2$.

Which of two nets is a real cube net? Six squares in a row won't fold
into a cube - the last two would overlap. A cross of four squares in
a column with one on each side of the second square folds up
perfectly.

## Common Mistake

Counting only three faces, or four. A box has *six* faces in three
equal pairs, and it's easy to forget the ones you can't see - the
bottom, the back. $ab + bc + ac$ is half the surface; the $2$ in front is
the other half. If the answer seems small, check that all six faces
are in.

## The Takeaway

A cuboid is six rectangular faces in three equal pairs, with $12$ edges
and $8$ vertices, described by length, width and height; a cube has all
edges equal. Its net is the six faces unfolded, and the surface area is
the sum of the six faces: $2(ab + bc + ac)$.

## Check Yourself

1. How many faces, edges and vertices does a cuboid have?
2. A cuboid is $6$ cm by $4$ cm by $3$ cm. Find its surface area.
3. Find the surface area of a cube with edge $7$ cm.
4. A cube has surface area $96\ \text{cm}^2$. Find its edge.
5. A cuboid's faces are $3 \times 5$, $5 \times 8$ and one more kind. What
   are the dimensions, and what's the third face?
6. Someone finds the surface of a $40 \times 30 \times 20$ box as $2600\
   \text{cm}^2$. What did they leave out?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $6$, $12$, $8$.
> 2. $2(24 + 12 + 18) = 108\ \text{cm}^2$.
> 3. $6 \cdot 49 = 294\ \text{cm}^2$.
> 4. $a^2 = 16$, $a = 4$ cm.
> 5. $3$, $5$, $8$; the third face is $3 \times 8$.
> 6. Half the faces - each pair was counted once; the area is $5200\
>    \text{cm}^2$.
