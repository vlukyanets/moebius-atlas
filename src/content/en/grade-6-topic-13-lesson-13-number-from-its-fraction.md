## Why It Matters

You've read $96$ pages and that's $\frac{2}{5}$ of the book - how long is
the book? A shop sold $\frac{3}{8}$ of its stock, which was $45$ jackets -
how many did it have? A part is known, and the fraction it makes of
the whole is known; the whole is what's wanted. It's the reverse of
"a fraction of a number," and with fraction division in hand it's a
single step.

## The Big Idea

If $\frac{2}{5}$ of the book is $96$ pages, then the book is what you
get by *undoing* the multiplication by $\frac{2}{5}$ - dividing by
$\frac{2}{5}$, which is multiplying by $\frac{5}{2}$: $96 \cdot \frac{5}{2}
= 240$ pages. The grade-5 way said the same thing in two moves: one
fifth is $96 : 2 = 48$, and five fifths are $48 \cdot 5 = 240$. Dividing
by the fraction does both moves at once.

The whole is bigger than the part, so the answer has to come out bigger
than $96$ - and dividing by a fraction less than $1$ is exactly what
makes numbers bigger.

## The Rule

If $\frac{a}{b}$ of an unknown number is $c$, the number is

$$
c : \frac{a}{b} = c \cdot \frac{b}{a}
$$

- divide the known part by the fraction. Percents are the same with a
  hundredth: if $p\%$ of a number is $c$, the number is $c : \frac{p}{100}$.

Check: the fraction of the answer should give the part back.

## Worked Example

$\frac{3}{8}$ of a shop's jackets were sold, and that was $45$ jackets.
How many jackets did the shop have?

- **The part and its fraction:** $45$ is $\frac{3}{8}$ of the whole.
- **Divide by the fraction:** $45 : \frac{3}{8} = 45 \cdot \frac{8}{3} =
  120$.
- **Check:** $\frac{3}{8}$ of $120$ is $120 \cdot \frac{3}{8} = 45$.

The shop had $120$ jackets.

With a decimal part: $\frac{4}{7}$ of a tank is $3.2$ litres. The tank is
$3.2 : \frac{4}{7} = 3.2 \cdot \frac{7}{4} = 5.6$ litres.

## Common Mistake

Multiplying by the fraction, as in the previous topic. $96 \cdot
\frac{2}{5} = 38.4$ pages - a "book" shorter than the part of it you've
read, which is absurd. "A fraction of a number" multiplies; "a number
from its fraction" divides. If the answer comes out smaller than the
part, the operation was backwards.

## The Takeaway

Knowing a part and what fraction of the whole it is, divide the part by
the fraction to get the whole - that's multiplying by the reciprocal.
The whole is always bigger than a proper part of it.

## Check Yourself

1. $\frac{3}{4}$ of a number is $36$. Find the number.
2. $\frac{5}{9}$ of a number is $\frac{10}{27}$. Find the number.
3. $\frac{2}{7}$ of a rope is $1.4$ m. How long is the rope?
4. $60\%$ of a class is $18$ pupils. How many pupils?
5. After spending $\frac{5}{8}$ of her money, Olha has $120$ hryvnias
   left. How much did she have? (Note: $120$ is $\frac{3}{8}$.)
6. Someone finds the book whose $\frac{2}{5}$ is $96$ pages as $38.4$
   pages. Why is that impossible?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $36 \cdot \frac{4}{3} = 48$.
> 2. $\frac{10}{27} \cdot \frac{9}{5} = \frac{2}{3}$.
> 3. $1.4 \cdot \frac{7}{2} = 4.9$ m.
> 4. $18 : 0.6 = 30$.
> 5. $120 : \frac{3}{8} = 320$ hryvnias.
> 6. The whole can't be shorter than its own part; it's $96 : \frac{2}{5}
>    = 240$ pages.
