## Why It Matters

Mix juice concentrate and water $1$ to $4$. The map is drawn at
$1 : 100\,000$. The team won $3$ games to $2$. A screen is $16 : 9$. All of
these compare two amounts by asking "how many times one is the other" -
not by how much they differ, but by their quotient. That comparison is
a ratio, and it's the idea behind proportions, scale, percents and
speed.

## The Big Idea

There are two ways to compare $12$ and $4$. Subtract, and $12$ is $8$
more than $4$. Divide, and $12$ is $3$ times $4$. The second one is the
ratio, and it's the one that survives scaling: double both amounts to
$24$ and $8$, and the difference becomes $16$ but the ratio is still $3$.
That's why recipes, maps and mixtures are written as ratios - they say
what stays the same when everything is made bigger or smaller.

## The Rule

The **ratio** of a number $a$ to a number $b$ (with $b \neq 0$) is the
quotient $a : b$, also written $\frac{a}{b}$. It shows how many times $a$
is bigger than $b$, or what part of $b$ the number $a$ is.

$$
12 : 4 = 3, \qquad 4 : 12 = \frac{1}{3}
$$

**Basic property of a ratio:** multiplying or dividing both terms of a
ratio by the same non-zero number doesn't change the ratio - it's the
basic property of a fraction:

$$
12 : 4 = 24 : 8 = 3 : 1
$$

This lets a ratio of fractions or decimals be turned into a ratio of
natural numbers. The ratio of two quantities of the same kind is found
after expressing them in the same unit.

## Worked Example

Simplify the ratio $2.4 : 0.9$, and find the ratio of $45$ cm to $1.5$ m.

- **$2.4 : 0.9$.** Multiply both by $10$ to clear the decimals:
  $24 : 9$. Divide both by $3$: $8 : 3$. So $2.4 : 0.9 = 8 : 3$ - the first
  is $\frac{8}{3}$ times the second.
- **$45$ cm to $1.5$ m.** Same unit first: $1.5$ m $= 150$ cm. Then
  $45 : 150$, divide by $15$: $3 : 10$. The first is $\frac{3}{10}$ of the
  second.

The juice: concentrate to water $1 : 4$ means the concentrate is
$\frac{1}{4}$ of the water - and $\frac{1}{5}$ of the whole drink.

## Common Mistake

Reading a ratio as a part of the whole. "Concentrate to water is $1 : 4$"
does not mean the drink is $\frac{1}{4}$ concentrate. The ratio compares
the two ingredients to *each other*; the whole is $1 + 4 = 5$ parts, and
the concentrate is $\frac{1}{5}$ of it. A ratio $a : b$ is $a$ against $b$,
never $a$ against $a + b$.

## The Takeaway

A ratio compares two numbers by division - how many times one is the
other. Both terms can be scaled by the same factor without changing
it, which is how it's simplified, and quantities must share a unit
before they're compared.

## Check Yourself

1. Find the ratio $36 : 12$ and $12 : 36$.
2. Simplify $1.5 : 4.5$.
3. Simplify $\frac{2}{3} : \frac{4}{9}$.
4. Find the ratio of $20$ minutes to $2$ hours.
5. A class has $12$ boys and $18$ girls. Find the ratio of boys to
   girls, and the ratio of boys to the whole class.
6. Someone says a $1 : 4$ juice mix is one quarter concentrate. What
   fraction is it really?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $3$ and $\frac{1}{3}$.
> 2. $15 : 45 = 1 : 3$.
> 3. $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{4} = \frac{3}{2}$, so $3 : 2$.
> 4. $20 : 120 = 1 : 6$.
> 5. $2 : 3$; $12 : 30 = 2 : 5$.
> 6. One fifth - $1$ part out of $1 + 4 = 5$.
