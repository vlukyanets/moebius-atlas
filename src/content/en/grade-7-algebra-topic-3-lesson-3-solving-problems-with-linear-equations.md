## Why It Matters

A cyclist leaves a town at $12$ km/h; two hours later a motorcyclist
follows at $36$ km/h - when do they meet? Two pipes fill a pool in $6$
and $12$ hours - how long together? A shop mixed $20\%$ and $50\%$ juice
into $3$ litres of $30\%$ - how much of each? Grade 6 built the routine:
name the unknown, translate, solve. Grade 7 turns it loose on the
classic problem types - motion, work, mixtures - where the equation is
always linear once you find it.

## The Big Idea

The plan hasn't changed: one unknown, everything else expressed
through it, one equality the story guarantees. What's new is knowing
where to look for that equality in each kind of story.

**Motion:** distances, speeds and times obey $s = vt$, and "they meet"
means "they've covered the same distance" or "their distances add up to
the gap." **Work:** the whole job is $1$, and a worker who finishes in
$6$ hours does $\frac{1}{6}$ per hour - rates add when people work
together. **Mixtures:** the amount of pure substance is conserved -
what went in is what's in the mix. Each of these gives one linear
equation.

## The Rule

To solve a word problem with a linear equation:

1. **Choose the unknown** $x$, with units, usually the quantity the
   question asks for or the smallest one.
2. **Express** every other quantity through $x$ - a table with a row per
   participant and columns for the related quantities helps.
3. **Write the equation** from the equality in the story.
4. **Solve** it and **check** the root against the story.
5. **Answer the question** - which may need one more step from $x$.

The standard equalities:

- motion: $s = vt$; meeting means equal distances, or distances summing
  to the separation;
- work: the whole job is $1$; rate $\times$ time $=$ part of the job done;
  rates of people working together add;
- mixtures and percents: the amount of pure substance before mixing
  equals the amount after.

## Worked Example

A cyclist leaves a town at $12$ km/h. Two hours later a motorcyclist
leaves the same town along the same road at $36$ km/h. How long after
starting does the motorcyclist catch the cyclist?

- **Unknown:** the motorcyclist's time, $x$ hours.
- **Express:** the cyclist has been riding $x + 2$ hours. Motorcyclist's
  distance: $36x$. Cyclist's distance: $12(x + 2)$.
- **Equality:** they meet on the road, so the distances are equal:
  $36x = 12(x + 2)$.
- **Solve:** $36x = 12x + 24$, $24x = 24$, $x = 1$.
- **Check:** in $1$ hour the motorcyclist goes $36$ km; the cyclist, in
  $3$ hours, $36$ km. Yes.

After $1$ hour.

Work: two pipes fill a pool in $6$ h and $12$ h. Together? Rates
$\frac{1}{6}$ and $\frac{1}{12}$ per hour; in $x$ hours together,
$\frac{x}{6} + \frac{x}{12} = 1$. Multiply by $12$: $2x + x = 12$, $x = 4$
hours.

## Common Mistake

Losing track of who has been going how long. In the chase, the
cyclist's time is not $x$ but $x + 2$ - he had a head start. Writing
$36x = 12x$ gives $x = 0$, a "meeting" at the start that never happened.
Put the times in a table before writing distances; the head start is a
row entry, not something to remember.

## The Takeaway

Motion, work and mixture problems each hand you one equality: equal or
summed distances, parts of one whole job, conserved pure substance.
Tabulate the participants, express everything through one unknown,
and the equation is linear.

## Check Yourself

1. Two cars leave towns $300$ km apart towards each other at $70$ km/h
   and $80$ km/h. When do they meet?
2. A worker finishes a job in $10$ days, another in $15$. How long
   together?
3. A boat goes $30$ km downstream and back in $4$ hours; the current is
   $2$ km/h. Find the boat's own speed. (Set up the equation; solving it
   needs a trick you'll meet later - just check that $v = 16$ works.)
4. How many grams of salt must be added to $200$ g of $10\%$ solution to
   make it $20\%$?
5. A number's double exceeds its half by $12$. Find it.
6. In the chase problem someone writes $36x = 12x$. What did they
   forget?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $70x + 80x = 300$, $x = 2$ hours.
> 2. $\frac{x}{10} + \frac{x}{15} = 1$, $x = 6$ days.
> 3. $\frac{30}{v + 2} + \frac{30}{v - 2} = 4$; with $v = 16$: $\frac{30}{18} +
>    \frac{30}{14} = \frac{5}{3} + \frac{15}{7} = 4$. Correct.
> 4. $20 + x = 0.2(200 + x)$, $x = 25$ g.
> 5. $2x - \frac{x}{2} = 12$, $x = 8$.
> 6. The cyclist's two-hour head start: his time is $x + 2$.
