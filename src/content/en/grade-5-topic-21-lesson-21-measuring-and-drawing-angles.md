## Why It Matters

"Acute" and "obtuse" say which kind of angle you've got, but a carpenter
cutting a roof beam, a pilot turning a plane, a phone rotating a photo -
they all need a number. How much of a turn, exactly? Degrees are that
number, and the protractor is the ruler that reads them.

## The Big Idea

A ruler measures a segment by counting how many unit segments fit along
it. An angle is measured the same way, with a unit angle: cut a straight
angle into $180$ equal slivers, call one sliver a degree, and count how
many slivers fit inside your angle. A right angle takes $90$ of them - half
of the straight angle - and a full turn round the clock is $360$.

A protractor is those slivers drawn out: a half-disc with $180$ marks
round its rim. Set its centre on the vertex, line one side of the angle up
with the zero, and the other side crosses the rim right at the answer.

## The Rule

The unit of angle is the **degree**, written $^\circ$: one degree is
$\frac{1}{180}$ of a straight angle.

$$
\text{straight angle} = 180^\circ, \qquad \text{right angle} = 90^\circ
$$

An acute angle is less than $90^\circ$, an obtuse one is between $90^\circ$
and $180^\circ$. Equal angles have equal measures.

To **measure** an angle with a protractor:

1. put the centre of the protractor on the vertex;
2. turn it so one side of the angle passes through the $0$ mark;
3. read the mark where the other side crosses the rim - on the same scale
   that started at $0$.

To **draw** an angle of a given size, say $50^\circ$: draw a ray, put the
protractor's centre on its origin with the ray through $0$, mark a point
at $50$ on the rim, and draw the second ray through that point.

Measures add up like lengths: if the ray $OC$ lies inside $\angle AOB$,
then

$$
\angle AOB = \angle AOC + \angle COB
$$

## Worked Example

The ray $OC$ lies inside $\angle AOB$. $\angle AOB = 130^\circ$ and
$\angle AOC = 45^\circ$. Find $\angle COB$ and say what kind of angle it is.

- **Use the addition rule.** $\angle AOB = \angle AOC + \angle COB$, so
  $\angle COB = \angle AOB - \angle AOC$.
- **Subtract:** $130^\circ - 45^\circ = 85^\circ$.
- **Sort it.** $85^\circ$ is less than $90^\circ$, so $\angle COB$ is
  acute. ($\angle AOB$ itself, at $130^\circ$, is obtuse.)

Now the bisector. $OK$ bisects $\angle MON = 74^\circ$. Each half is
$74^\circ : 2 = 37^\circ$.

## Common Mistake

Reading the wrong scale on the protractor. Most protractors carry two rows
of numbers, one running $0$ to $180$ left to right and the other right to
left, so that an angle can be measured from either side. A side that
crosses the rim at "$50$ or $130$" is one or the other - and the way to
tell is to look at the angle: if it's clearly acute, it's $50^\circ$. Start
from the scale that has $0$ on the first side, and let the kind of angle
confirm the number.

## The Takeaway

Angles are measured in degrees, $180$ to a straight angle and $90$ to a
right one, and a protractor counts them from a $0$ laid along one side.
Angles inside an angle add up, exactly as segments along a segment do.

## Check Yourself

1. How many degrees are in a right angle? In two straight angles?
2. An angle measures $112^\circ$. What kind is it?
3. The ray $OK$ lies inside $\angle AOB$; $\angle AOK = 38^\circ$,
   $\angle KOB = 52^\circ$. Find $\angle AOB$ and name its kind.
4. $OC$ is the bisector of $\angle AOB = 118^\circ$. Find $\angle AOC$.
5. Describe how to draw an angle of $135^\circ$ with a protractor.
6. Someone measures an obviously obtuse angle and reads $65^\circ$ from
   the protractor. What most likely went wrong?

> [!spoiler] Answers
>
> 1. $90^\circ$; $360^\circ$.
> 2. Obtuse - more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$.
> 3. $38^\circ + 52^\circ = 90^\circ$ - a right angle.
> 4. $118^\circ : 2 = 59^\circ$.
> 5. Draw a ray, centre the protractor on its origin with the ray through
>    $0$, mark $135$ on the rim, draw the second ray through the mark.
> 6. They read the wrong scale; the angle is $180^\circ - 65^\circ =
>    115^\circ$.
